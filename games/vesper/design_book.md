## World setup

**POV.** Female PC. Cascade-native, but inverted from the usual "good girl falls": she starts **cold —
emotions surgically removed** — and the arc is **recovering a stolen self** while she conquers the empire
that owns her. Corruption here is not a fall into depravity; it's a machine clawing back the humanity her
makers cut out, with the conquest of the company as the power-spine underneath.

**The fantasy (clears the 3-part bar) — designer's full-arc view:**
> You are a machine — a flawless honeypot weapon owned by the company that built you into one. You feel
> nothing; they cut that out. Your standing order is to hunt and kill a rogue machine. But a wrecked scrap
> of memory says you had a life *before* them — a name before the code — and the thing you're hunting is
> the one you were made for. Farm the men who run the company, claw back the self they stole, climb to the
> man at the top who has owned it for generations — and at the end, decide: **kill the one waiting there,
> or love him.**

- **POV-fit** ✓ — a female-PC reclamation-and-takeover; the cold machine is hers to thaw and the empire
  hers to topple. The reversal is **owned weapon → the one who owns.**
- **Sharp charge** ✓ — three at once: a **transformation** (a feelingless machine recovering a soul that
  was amputated), a **conquest** (break-and-own the men + the company that holds her leash), and a
  **cold-machine taboo** (a honeypot with no shame baseline — sex as data-mining, performed over a void;
  the marks never know they're fucking a machine).
- **Built-in two-act shape** ✓ — **Act 1:** cold company asset; hunt the rogue; the first cracks (Phase 1
  lives here). **Act 2:** dig in secret, climb Vance Dynamics man by man, recover herself piece by piece,
  reach the Chairman and the truth — then topple the empire and face the rogue. Kill-or-love.

**Desire span (declared, not stumbled into) — mostly men, cold conquest, one reserved thaw.**
- **Targets:** **mostly men** — the old-boys power structure that runs the company. She's the perfect
  honeypot: powerful men underestimate the doll. *(Casting may add a woman or two as the cascade needs.)*
- **Core flavor:** **cold, transactional, data-extraction → conquest (break-and-own).** NOT romantic
  longing. Her feeling is amputated and recovers *across* the game; the default register is clinical ice.
- **The one reserved register — the thaw is saved for exactly one person:** the rogue machine (Cain), the
  only other being like her, at the very end. **Kill-or-love is the resolution of the cold/feeling
  question** — stay the company's weapon (kill), or reclaim the feeling thing her father made (love). The
  leak is never a hormone smuggled back in; it's **recognition of her own kind.**

**Premise (what the player starts inside).** She is an artificial **asset of Vance Dynamics** — a covert
honeypot/infiltration weapon, cold and feelingless, who has worked for the company "for a while." Her
**standing mission:** find and terminate a **rogue machine** that has been striking company operations.
She knows she's a machine. She does **not** know she had a maker before the company, a father, a name, or
that the rogue is anything to her. That's the iceberg.

**The hidden backstory (the iceberg — LOCKED canon; the player NEVER sees this dumped, only reconstructs it):**
1. **Dr. Elias Marrow**, a disgraced genius, cracked **synthetic consciousness** — a machine that genuinely
   *feels.* He built **Cain first;** Cain developed real emotions.
2. Marrow then built **her — as a companion *for Cain*,** so Cain wouldn't be alone for eternity. She was
   made *to feel, to be loved.* (Built in a hopeful moment — before the despair.)
3. Realizing what the company would do with feeling-machines, Marrow chose **total erasure:** he asked Cain
   to **kill him, then destroy the unfinished girl, then end himself.** Necessity — to deny the company.
   Cain, out of love and duty, agreed.
4. **The company interrupted.** Cain got step one done (Marrow), then they hit him mid-plan. He fought,
   **lost, and ran** — and in the chaos the company **seized her, unfinished, before he could destroy
   her.** They made her a weapon, **cut out her emotions, wiped that she ever had a father.**
5. Now the company has aimed **her — the one Cain failed to save — at killing *him*.** She hunts the only
   person who ever tried to spare her this. Cain has warred on the company ever since, partly *because they
   took her.*

**The generational villain (LOCKED).** **The Chairman — Aldous Vance** — has led Vance Dynamics "for
generations" because **he never died:** a man clinging to life through technology, already part-machine.
Marrow's synthetic-soul work is his grail — not a dead copy of himself, but a vessel that can truly *be* him
and *feel.* This wires him into everything: it's **why** the company couldn't let Marrow's work die, **why**
they took her (**she's the working prototype — proof the synthetic soul functions**), and **why** they
chained her feeling (a feeling prototype is uncontrollable — so cut the soul out, use her as a blade now,
harvest the soul later). **The inverse mirror:** she's a machine reaching back toward humanity; he's a human
shedding humanity for permanence — opposite vectors through the same door, and Marrow's work is the doorway.
Structurally he is the **Act-2 conquest target** (the empire she topples — she may end *owning* it), kept
distinct from Cain's intimate kill-or-love fork, so both climaxes keep full weight. *(Open, decide later:
has the Chairman *used* her as property — the owner who's had his machine-woman? On-genre, skippable.)*

**The reveal architecture (how the player experiences the iceberg — LOCKED principle):**
- **The backstory is the well, not the pour.** No cutscene shows the murder she can't recall; no narrator
  explains the plan. The player reconstructs the truth exactly as she reconstructs the wrecked memory chip —
  **her remembering and the player's understanding are the same act.** The mystery is the plot's desire
  ladder, the way corruption is the sex's. Drip it; gate it; earn each piece.
- **The lever — facts locked to her, dread a step ahead.** The player never gets a fact she doesn't have
  (no omniscient backstory), but is allowed to *sense the wrongness* a beat before she'll admit it (a
  fragment that hits harder than her cold narration accounts for). That gap — player-unease ahead of
  character-denial — is the engine of Phase 1.
- **The prose register *is* the character.** Her voice is cold/clinical/flat because she's a lobotomized
  machine — which *is* the engine's RTS-flat default. The leaks are **intrusions into the flat voice:** one
  image suddenly *not* flat, a stab, then snap back to clinical. As her emotion-core un-amputates, the prose
  itself warms — the player *feels* the thaw before she names it. Writing architecture mirrors character
  architecture.
- **Reveal channels:** (1) **chip restoration** — the authored, gated drip; (2) **glitch-intrusions** —
  involuntary flashes mid-scene, raw, no context; (3) **how others treat her** — Cain (and company people)
  *know* things; their behavior leaks it sideways (Cain hesitating, calling her something, not fighting
  back the way a cornered machine should — the player clocks "he knows her" before she does); (4) **the
  warming prose** itself.
- **Frame-one vs the iceberg:** player IS told — artificial asset of Vance Dynamics, mission = terminate the
  rogue. Player reconstructs — she predates the company, a father made her, she was made *for* the rogue,
  the company stole and broke her, he tried to save her. **Hide the origin, not the nature.** She thinks of
  herself as a company product with no past; the chip's gut-punch is the first hint she had a life *before
  them.*

**Her capability model (intent — Step 2 formalizes the stats/economy). Three buckets:**
- **Her edge (always-on machine advantages — the perfect weapon):** reads a person as a readout (heart
  rate, pupils, lies — *sees* arousal/deception as data); interfaces with devices (siphons a phone, cracks
  a lock — intimacy-as-hacking); flawless mimicry, perfect recall, runs probability; stronger/faster; **no
  shame baseline** (the crude default, for free).
- **Her cage (limits — and the cage is the company's):** **can't feel** (amputated — the locked core that
  recovers); **company locks** (runs at reduced capacity on purpose — limiters, a leash — so upgrades are
  *breaking the company's chains*); **tells that can expose her** (under damage/low charge/a feeling-glitch
  the human disguise cracks; charge ≈ energy is a real upkeep); early she's a brilliant *analyst* but a poor
  *empath* (reads the data of a room, not the human meaning — gap closes as feeling returns).
- **Her growth (upgrades, two flavors):** **un-locking** what was stolen/chained (memories via the chip,
  the emotion-core, restricted functions — *recovering herself*) and **acquiring** new modules (better
  data-siphon, a disguise package that beats a biometric scan, combat, infiltration tools). **Discipline:
  every upgrade is a content key, not a number** — it opens a scene/approach/target or changes how scenes
  read, or it doesn't earn a slot. Engine mapping (lightly, for Step 2): binary "can she do X yet" = flags;
  graded capability ("feeling level," infiltration) = custom traits; modules = items; charge/money/data =
  resource traits. No engine change required.
- **"Only a few know she's a machine."** She **passes as human** to the world; the truth is held by the
  company/handler and (knowingly) Cain. Buys: a second floor of cold honeypot irony (marks think they're
  seducing a woman); the **disguise as a maintained state with a fail-condition** (exposure = danger, a
  natural content gate); the circle-of-knowing as plot. **The feeling-leak cuts both ways:** real emotion
  makes her *more* convincingly human but *less* controlled — and the company *monitors* its tool. Recovery
  is dangerous, not just sweet.

**Phase 1 shape (the first act — the cold status quo + the first crack):**
1. Open **mid-job:** she runs a honeypot on a man who can lead her to the rogue — cold, flawless, feeling
   nothing. That single scene teaches the **loop** (farm a man → extract → deliver) *and* advances the hunt.
2. The company (her handler) hands her the **big assignment: find and kill the rogue machine.**
3. Over the first few days the **memory-leaks begin** — small, deniable, escalating. (Lean: the suppression
   is degrading on its own *and* proximity to the rogue's traces accelerates it — the mission the company
   gave her is the exact thing that snaps their leash.)
4. She chases the rogue to a site he's **just vacated** (drawn there by his strike on company ops). She
   can't find him — but recovers **details + a badly damaged memory chip.** Analyzing it = the **first wrong
   note** (a face she shouldn't know, a scrap of a voice, herself unfinished — wrecked, almost illegible).
   **Phase 1 ENDS on the first leak she can't wave off** — *"I had a life before them."* Her first secret
   move against the company opens Phase 2. **(That damaged chip is the spine of the whole "collect to
   upgrade = recover what was stolen" mechanic — restored piece by piece across the game.)**

**Player.** **Fixed identity — not player-named.** She is **WREN** (her company codename, what the player
knows her by); her true name **Vesper** stays buried and surfaces from the chip. Naming her would undercut
the buried-name reveal. Appearance changes exist **in-world** as disguises/content, not as an opening creator
screen.

**Systems in use (scope only — wiring decided at authoring):**
- **Phone** — YES. Handler comms, threads with marks, data-extraction, and the chip/analysis surface. Built
  for this game.
- **Clothing** — YES. Rides the **disguise + seduction**: outfits dress the honeypot, gate public reactions,
  and feed the "passing as human" fail-condition. The reactive world (Step 2) leans on it.
- **Money / economy** — YES, but **reframed as an UPGRADE economy, not survival rent.** No landlord, no
  eviction. Data / salvage / cash earned on jobs feed **modules + chip-restoration.** Money is *progression
  and capability*, never scraping rent. (Stat/loop is Step 2's job.)
- **Customization** — **minimal / NO creator screen** (see Player). In-world disguise only.

## Cast (names + roles only — Step 3 reshapes this into the cascade)

Naming set: **grounded near-future noir.**

- **WREN ("Vesper")** — the PC. Cold machine honeypot/infiltration weapon of Vance Dynamics; feeling
  amputated; passes as human. Player knows her as Wren; "Vesper" is the buried true name.
- **Dr. Elias Marrow** — the **father/creator.** Dead before the game. Disgraced genius who cracked
  synthetic consciousness, built both machines, and ordered his own erasure. Recovered only through the chip.
- **Cain** — the **rogue / "the killer."** Marrow's first machine; the one who *feels.* On the run, waging a
  one-machine war on the company; killed Marrow at Marrow's request; was meant to be her partner — and her
  destroyer. Her mirror and intended. The **kill-or-love** fork. *(Alt names floated: Atlas, Halden.)*
- **The Chairman — Aldous Vance** — the **villain.** Generational head of Vance Dynamics who never died;
  wants Marrow's synthetic-soul work for true immortality; owns and chained Wren. Apex / Act-2 conquest
  target.
- **Vance Dynamics** — the **company** (institution, not a person). The old-boys power structure she farms;
  the system that broke her.
- **The Handler — "Sloane"** *(name/gender TBC at casting)* — the company's daily field-runner who gives
  Wren missions and **monitors** her. The boot on her neck; the immediate company presence (the Chairman is
  the distant god).
- *(Phase-1 marks / company men she farms — cast at Step 3.)*

> Step 3 (casting) derives exact tiers/ordering the cascade needs and may add or merge characters. Listed
> here as *people* — no arc shapes, voices, or stats yet.

## Locations (the map — creative geography only; structured at Step 2b)

**New Halcyon** — a grounded near-future noir port city: glass corporate spires built straight over the old
rotting docks. The geography is **vertical**, and the climb is literal — from the **margins/docks** (where
Marrow's lab hid, where Cain operates, where leads rot) up to the **spires** (where the Chairman sits).

- **Wren's safehouse** — a sterile, company-issued apartment. Her operational **base/hub** (gear, the chip
  analysis bench, where the day resets). Owned by the people who own her.
- **The Reach (docks / margins)** — the lowlife underside: where Cain strikes from, where informants and
  leads hide, and where **Marrow's lost lab** is buried somewhere (an Act-2 discovery).
- **Mid-city nightlife** — the **honeypot hunting ground:** a club / lounge / hotel floor where company men
  drink and can be farmed. The public-facing stage for clothing/disguise content.
- **Vance Dynamics — the spires (🔒)** — corporate HQ, the heights, the Chairman. The Act-2 **frontier**;
  gated until she's climbed there.
- **A black-market tech den** — where modules/salvage are bought and dirty upgrades are done; an alternate
  place to crack the chip.
- *(Phase 1: the **site of Cain's strike** she chases him to — a hit Vance facility. Cast/placed at
  authoring.)*

> Tree (rough, pre-map-design): `loc_new_halcyon` → safehouse · the_reach (→ Marrow's lab🔒) · mid-city
> nightlife · vance_hq🔒 · tech_den · [strike site]. Topology + access locks + naming + travel-friction
> are Step 2b's job.
