ABOUT THIS BOOK
───────────────

This book describes the complete game design for "Two Weeks" — a single-NPC
forbidden romance game built according to the v6 Game Design Book prompt and
v3 TOML Generation prompt. Rewritten from the ground up from v1.0.

This is Phase 1 of 6. Each phase builds on the previous.

## 1. THE NPC

**Name**: Ethan
**Role**: Step-Brother / Love Interest
**Age**: Late 20s
**Vibe**: Protective older brother energy with suppressed desire underneath. Warm,
genuinely kind, conflict-avoidant. The type who keeps the peace at his own expense.
Not a predator — a man caught between duty and desire.

**What makes him appealing?**
- He *remembers*. Small things — her favorite meal, how she takes her coffee, that
  summer they got too close. His memory is his love language.
- He's physically imposing (tall, athletic) but emotionally gentle. The contrast
  between his body and his hesitation creates tension.
- His loyalty is both his most attractive quality and the obstacle. He's the kind
  of man who would stay in a wrong marriage out of duty — which makes the moments
  he chooses desire over duty feel genuinely earned.
- He doesn't initiate. He restrains himself until he can't anymore. The player has
  to push, and when he finally breaks, it's intense precisely because he held back
  so long.

## 2. THE SETTING

**Type**: Domestic — family home
**The House**: A suburban family home where both characters grew up as step-siblings
for 8 years. Now it's Ethan's home (he stayed after the parents moved). Every room
holds memories. The physical space itself is a character — hallways with family photos,
the kitchen where they had midnight snacks, the pool where they spent summers, the
bedroom doors that were always too close together.

**Why this setting works for FORBIDDEN driver:**
- Every room has innocent family memories that make the forbidden feelings worse
- Shared spaces force proximity (one kitchen, one bathroom, one couch)
- No escape — she's staying in the house for 2 weeks
- Madison's presence lingers even when she's absent (her things on the dresser, her
  engagement photo on the nightstand)
- The house itself normalizes intimacy (it's "just family living together")

## 3. THE RELATIONSHIP

**Type**: Step-siblings
**History**: Parents married when player was ~12 and Ethan was ~14. They grew up in
the same house for 8 years. Feelings developed during high school but were never
acted on. Player left for college across the country partly to escape them. She hasn't
been home in 2 years.

**Current situation**: Ethan is engaged to Madison. Wedding is in exactly 14 days.
Madison is away with her family for the first 12 days, finalizing arrangements.
Player arrives for the wedding. They're alone in the house.

**Why step-sibling works:**
- Not blood-related — the taboo is social/familial, not biological
- Shared history creates genuine emotional foundation (this isn't strangers)
- The "family" label makes every romantic feeling feel like a betrayal
- There's a second layer of forbidden: he's engaged to someone else

## 4. DRIVER ANALYSIS

**Primary Driver: FORBIDDEN (tension)**

This is the right primary because the game's core tension isn't "will they fall in
love?" (they already have) — it's "will they act on what's forbidden?"

The taboo operates on TWO levels:
1. **Step-sibling taboo** — "He's family. This is wrong."
2. **Engagement taboo** — "He belongs to someone else. This is betrayal."

The T1-T8+ progression for FORBIDDEN maps perfectly to this story:

| Tier | FORBIDDEN Driver | Two Weeks Mapping |
|------|------------------|-------------------|
| T1 | Awareness of why this is wrong | Arrival — "just here for the wedding" |
| T2 | Stolen glances, thrilling because forbidden | Old photos, sleepless nights |
| T3 | Almost getting caught, near misses | The Couch, Almost Kiss |
| T4 | Risky groping, "Someone might see" | Post-confession, hallway encounters |
| T5 | Risky manual, thrill of being caught | Escalating touches, stolen moments |
| T6 | Secret oral, "We can't keep doing this" | Shower encounters, kitchen at 3 AM |
| T7 | The taboo fully embraced, first forbidden sex | First Night Together |
| T8+ | Risk is part of the thrill, all positions | Madison in the house, garage, night before wedding |

**Secondary Driver: LOVE (affection)**

The love driver adds emotional weight. Without it, this would be pure taboo
exploitation. The love driver ensures:
- Dialogue has emotional depth, not just "we shouldn't"
- The relationship feels earned, not just transgressive
- Endings carry genuine weight (this isn't just about sex — it's about choosing love)
- Activities build genuine connection alongside forbidden tension

**Combined Driver Tone:**
"I love you. I've always loved you. And I hate that I love you, because it means
I'm about to destroy everything I'm supposed to be."

## 5. CORE PREMISE

**The "What if?" hook:**
What if you returned home after two years for your step-brother's wedding — only
to discover the feelings you thought you'd buried were still alive, and he feels
the same way? You have exactly 14 days before he marries someone else. Every moment
is a choice between what's right and what you truly want.

**The emotional journey:**
- **Days 1-3**: Denial. "I'm over it. This is fine. He's getting married." But the
  proximity, the memories, the way he still remembers everything about her — the
  feelings come flooding back.
- **Days 4-7**: Acknowledgment. They can't pretend anymore. The tension breaks into
  confession, almost-kisses, loaded silences. The question shifts from "do we feel
  this?" to "what do we do about it?"
- **Days 8-10**: Crossing the line. First kiss. First night. The forbidden fully
  embraced. But the clock is ticking. Madison calls. The wedding approaches.
- **Days 11-12**: Addiction. Can't stay away. The relationship deepens, becomes
  desperate. They're building something real while a countdown destroys it.
- **Days 13-14**: Confrontation. Madison arrives. Reality crashes in. The night before
  the wedding. Wedding morning. The player's choices and stats determine which of 4
  endings plays out.

**What makes this different from a generic forbidden romance:**
- The **14-day deadline** creates urgency. There's no "maybe someday." The clock is
  always visible.
- The **engagement** means this isn't just "forbidden love" — it's betrayal of a
  real person (Madison) who is genuinely nice, making the player feel the moral weight.
- The **shared history** means the love is real and deep, not just lust or novelty.
  These two people have 8 years of memories.
- **Guilt as a game mechanic** — Ethan's guilt stat directly affects which ending
  you get. Too much guilt and he can't leave Madison even if he loves the player.
  The player must manage the emotional cost of their pursuit.

## 6. TONE

**Type**: Slow-burn (14 days)
**Escalation pace**: Days 1-3 are purely emotional. First physical contact (beyond
hugs) doesn't happen until Day 5-6 at earliest. First kiss around Day 8-9.
Sex around Day 10 if stats are high enough. This gives the player time to feel
the emotional build — the sex is meaningful because of what came before it.

**Tonal balance:**
| Element | Weight | Description |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| FORBIDDEN | 40% | Step-sibling tension, engagement betrayal, "we shouldn't" |
| EMOTIONAL | 35% | Rekindled love, shared history, impossible choices, guilt |
| ROMANTIC | 25% | Genuine connection, tenderness, "I've always loved you" |

**What this is NOT:**
- Not a corruption game (neither character is being corrupted — they're giving in to
  pre-existing feelings)
- Not a seduction game (neither is manipulating the other — the desire is mutual)
- Not a power game (they're equals, neither dominates)
- It IS a "two people who love each other facing an impossible situation" game

**Stat system (3 stats):**
| Stat | Owner | Start | Purpose |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| affection | npc_ethan | 15 | Primary progression gate (FORBIDDEN + LOVE driver) |

**Gate flag system (to be designed in Phase 2):**
Story events will set unlock flags that gate activity tiers. Preliminary mapping:

| Gate Flag | Set By (Story Event) | Unlocks (Activity Tier) |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| lingering_touch_unlock | Old Photos / Sleepless Night | T2: Suggestive touches |
| flirt_unlock | The Couch / Confession | T3: Intentional flirtation |
| kiss_unlock | First Kiss | T4: Kissing, intimate touching |
| intimacy_unlock | First Night Together | T5: Full intimate content |

These flags + affection thresholds together gate every activity tier (dual gating).

## 8. GAME ARCHITECTURE

**Architecture: Single-NPC Romance (Option A)**

This game uses the Single-NPC Romance architecture because:
- One primary NPC (Ethan) with one deep relationship arc
- Primary stat lives on the NPC (affection)
- Player builds ONE relationship through activities + story events
- No parallel arcs, no shared unlock flags across NPCs
- The 14-day structure is inherently linear, not branching

**Relevant v6 sections for Phase 2+:**
- NPC Driver Table (FORBIDDEN + LOVE)
- Emotional Quadrant progression
- Hybrid Gating Model (stat threshold + flag)
- Single-NPC gate flag design
- Activity tier structure with conditional choices

**NOT needed (Multi-NPC specific):**
- Player-stat-driven progression (corruption as primary)
- Shared unlock flags across arcs
- Staggered corruption bands
- NPC Trait Triangle (love/trust/corruption per NPC)
- Clothing/wardrobe system
- Economic pressure / rent system

★ RECOMMENDED DIRECTION:

Two Weeks is a 14-day slow-burn forbidden romance using the FORBIDDEN + LOVE
driver combination. The player (step-sister, early 20s) returns home for her
step-brother Ethan's wedding. They're alone in the family home for 12 days
before the fiancée arrives.

The game's core tension operates on a ticking clock. Every interaction builds
toward a deadline that can't be moved. The 3-stat system (affection / boldness /
guilt) creates 4 distinct endings — from "He Chooses You" (high affection, high
boldness, low guilt) to "What Could Have Been" (insufficient investment).

Key design priorities for Phase 2+:
1. Design gate flags that create a clear "narrative permission" chain for
   physical escalation — every activity tier requires both a stat AND a flag
2. Build activities as proper tiered-choice canvases (not separate tier canvases)
3. Use guilt as a meaningful stat, not just a counter — it should create
   genuine tension in dialogue and affect which choices feel available
4. The 14-day timeline means stat math must be tight — the player needs to
   reach affection 90+ by Day 10 to see the best content, requiring ~3
   activities per day at average +3 per activity
