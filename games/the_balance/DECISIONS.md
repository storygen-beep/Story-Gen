# 1. Four questions for you

The only part of this page that needs you. My answer is there — overrule any of them.

### Q1 · Do the meters live on **her**, or on **each person**?  — ANSWERED

**On her.** Written up in *What the game tracks about her*, which is the page to overrule it on.

She has four and everyone reads them. The other way gives all eight their own set, and then the
game is eight climbs that never touch and nothing ever changes her.

*Why it matters:* every gate in the game hangs off this. Five of our past games never picked, and
landed in a muddle between the two.

### Q2 · Does the police officer stay in the cast?  — ANSWERED

**He stays, and he's been simplified.** A strict officer who wants nothing from her. He used to be
one of the men paying for her stream, which also had him paying a seventeen-year-old — that's gone.

*Why it matters:* he's the only man in the game she can't trade with, which is what the police
station is for.

### Q3 · The money — $2,400 a semester, $150 a week, ~$220 earned a week

She owes Gil $2,400. She pays $150 a week. An honest week at the cafe pays about $220.
So she survives on clean work and never gets ahead.

**My answer: hold these.**

*Why it matters:* these are guesses until we price the actual jobs. Eight of our past
ten games set this wrong — the debt was so small a player cleared a whole week in half
a day, and nothing ever squeezed.

### Q4 · Does the phone ship in the first release, or the second?

**My answer: first release — messages and the streaming app.** Nobody subscribes to her: people
follow, and each stream pays on the night.

*Why it matters:* the stream is the spine of the game. Two of her four meters only move
there.

### Three rules about Gil — now built in, not written down

He's both the man she owes **and** someone she can end up with. Normally that's a mistake — the game
answers "how do I pay him?" with "in bed," and then the cafe, the bar and the stream are pointless.

**The three rules that stop it are no longer rules. They're how the game is built:**

| the rule | how it's enforced |
|---|---|
| He never asks for sex | **His ladder has no steps he can open.** Every step is opened by her, not him. There's no mechanism for him to start anything |
| She can clear the debt with cash | The jobs' honest weekly total beats the weekly payment. Arithmetic, checked once |
| He's never the main sex character | The build prints his share every time. Third or fourth, never first |

**Better than written rules**, because a future session can't break them by accident — there's
nothing there to break.

**He's strict and he doesn't drink.** In by ten, door stays open, chores first. That's who he is,
so it lives in his description rather than here. A drunk step dad hands her an excuse for
everything.

### One thing that stays off-limits

**Lynn — the mother — is never touched.** She's seen through a door and that's all.
Written down here so a later release doesn't quietly read it as an oversight.

# 5. Words we can't use

Checked every noun against 27 shipped games in this genre before naming anything.

**Rejected — nobody in the genre uses them:** undeclared · bursar · registrar · enrollment ·
assistantship · seminar · camgirl · livestream · laundromat · stockroom · carrel.

**Also:** the genre writes **step dad**, **step sister**, **step brother** as two words.
Never *stepdad*.

**Caught in the Want before it spread:** cheque → **check** · recognising → **recognizing** ·
promotions → **promotion** · runners → **joggers**. British spellings put five of our past
games in a dialect this genre doesn't use.
