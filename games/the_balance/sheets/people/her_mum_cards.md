## Eating

Seven rows, and they are not one scene. Six different meals, and every one is at its own hour, so
the card can tell them apart.

| hour | days | her |
|---|---|---|
| **07:30** | Mon Wed Fri | eating standing up, one eye on the clock. She has twenty minutes too |
| **08:00** | Tue Thu Sat | in off the ward, still in uniform, eating without tasting it |
| **08:30** | Sun | at the table with whoever got up |
| **11:15** | Mon Wed Fri | putting the shopping away, tins first |
| **12:30** | Mon Wed Fri · Sun | sat down to eat for half an hour |
| **15:15** | Tue Thu Sat | a late lunch on her own |

**What the player can do:**

1. **Sit down and eat with her.** Half an hour.
2. **Put it away with her.** The 11:15 hour only, when it's shopping and not a meal. Half an hour.
3. **Take yours and go.** Five minutes.
4. **Leave her to it.**

The chores page already decided the shape of this: *"Eating is people at one table. That makes it a
card — sit down and eat with them — the way the opening already has 'Sit down and eat.' There is no
hunger meter, so what eating gives her is the people at the table."*

**The two that are not like the others.** At 08:00 on a Tuesday she has come straight off a night
shift and is eating in her uniform without tasting it. At 15:15 she is eating alone, hours after
everyone else. Those are the two worth writing properly.

## Sat down

Sixteen rows, and **every one of them exists because the player took a chore off her.**

Say *"I'll do it"* on the laundry and she goes and sits in the front room. The game already moves
her. Until now there was nothing there when she arrived. This is the card that makes taking the work
off her worth anything.

Four scenes, by the hour:

| band | rows | her |
|---|---|---|
| **Morning**, seven to half eleven | 5 | sat down early, which she never is |
| **Afternoon**, twelve to quarter past five | 6 | the middle of the day with nothing in it |
| **Her hour off**, quarter past five to six, Tue Thu Sat | 1 | the one hour her own week gives her |
| **Evening**, six to half eight | 4 | sat down while the house is still going |

Underneath each, two or three ways of saying it, so a card she'll see a dozen times a week doesn't
read the same every time.

**What the player can do:**

1. **Sit down with her.** Half an hour.
2. **Leave her to it.**

**Her hour off is the one to get right.** It is the only row in her week that her own page calls
hers. Every other sat-down row is an hour the player bought her.

## Sunday afternoon

One row. **Half one to six on a Sunday — four and a half hours, the longest single window in her
week.** *"In the chair with the TV on, the one afternoon she gets."*

Its own card, not part of *sat down*, because it runs straight through the weekday afternoon hours
and the card can't tell them apart otherwise.

**What the player can do:**

1. **Sit down with her.** An hour, and she can do it again — there are four and a half.
2. **Leave her to it.**

**This is a straight choice and it should feel like one.** Sunday has no class and all five shifts
are open. Four and a half hours with her mum is four and a half hours not earning.

## Out on the strip

One row. **Monday, Wednesday and Friday, nine to eleven.** Her mum in town with a list, in the coat
and shoes she only wears out.

**The only place in the game the player meets her outside this house.**

Two things make it worth a card:

- **Class is nine to twelve, Monday to Thursday.** So Monday and Wednesday she is sitting in a
  lecture while her mum is on the strip. **Friday is the only day she can be there without skipping**
  — the week page already says it: *"Friday — no class."*
- **The cafe is on the strip**, midway along. The ten o'clock shift runs ten to one. So on a Friday
  she can be behind the counter while her mum walks past the window with the shopping.

**What the player can do:**

1. **Walk round the shops with her.** An hour.
2. **Say hello and let her get on.** Five minutes.
3. **Leave her to it.**

## What they pay

**Nothing.**

The chores page settled it: *"No $5, because it's her mum's job. What she gets is time with her
mum."* These four cost half an hour or an hour and give no money and move no meter.

That is the point of them. Everything else in this game is an hour bought with something. These are
the hours that aren't.

## What it keeps track of

**Nothing.** Nothing here climbs, nothing resets overnight, nothing is counted.

## The eight cards that already exist

Her chore cards — breakfast, the dishes, lunch, dinner, laundry, dusting — are in the game and each
one has **exactly one paragraph.** The laundry card comes up seven times a week and says the same
sentence every time.

**Each gets two more ways of saying it.** Same card, same hours, same choices. Just not the same
words on the third Tuesday.
