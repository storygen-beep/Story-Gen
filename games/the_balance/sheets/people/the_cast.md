## Gil — her step dad

- **Wants** his house run his way, and to never be thanked for the tuition. He's strict, he doesn't
  drink, and he has never once mentioned what he paid.
- **Works for himself, out of the van.** A trade, and the garage is the workshop — the tools are on
  the wall in the order they're drawn on it, and the list on the fridge is in the same handwriting
  as the ones he writes for himself. **This is the answer to the one odd thing about him:** a man who
  counts five dollars a chore handed over $2,400 in cash without mentioning it. He's had good years.
  He's also never had anybody to send an invoice to about a bad day.
- **Found** in the garage at five with the van open, the kitchen until eight and back in it by six,
  and upstairs by nine.
- **Gives** chore money, lifts, and the debt itself.
- **Says no by** saying nothing and tightening the rules.
- **He never opens a step.** Every step with him is one she opens. There is no move he can make on
  his own, which is the whole reason the game can have him at the kitchen table.

## Nate — her step brother, 20, the year above her at the same college

- **Wants** her out of his way. He was fine before she moved in and he's never pretended otherwise.
- **Underneath, he wants her, and hiding it is the only thing he's good at.** Her climb with him is
  making him show it. The day she walks in on him is where it first cracks.
- **Found** on campus most weekdays, home in the evenings and at odd hours.
- **Gives** nothing willingly. Everything from him is taken or traded for — and on campus he can
  make her look like nobody in front of people who matter.
- **Says no by** getting worse about it. He tells Gil about the chores, and he's still in the next room
  tomorrow.

## Tasha — her step sister, 21

- **Wants** an audience, and she'll happily make Nell into one.
- **Found** out until four, in after that, gone Friday and Saturday nights.
- **Gives** clothes, parties, and advice that isn't wrong.
- **Says no by** laughing at her. **She cannot out her** — she doesn't know, and she's an example,
  not a threat.

## Lynn — her mum

- **Wants** her daughter to be fine, and she is too tired to check.
- **Found** on the ward three nights a week, asleep by ten on the others.
- **Gives** the hours. Everything in this game happens while she's out or asleep.
- **Never touched.** She's the price, not a person to be won.

## Owen — the cafe owner

- **Wants** her, and he pays in hours rather than cash.
- **Is a strict boss first.** Exact about the work, quick to notice sloppy, and he hands out nothing
  for nothing. The office isn't locked; what she walks in there for is the question.
- **Gives** shifts, the move up, and eventually the keys.
- **Says no by** giving the shift to somebody else. He never threatens, and that's what makes it
  land.

## Hale — her professor

- **Wants** to be the one who shaped her.
- **Teaches one of her four subjects**, sets its homework and grades its exams himself. So the lever
  on her is her marks, and marks need attendance, and attendance needs sleep. **The other three
  teachers are names on a timetable** until one of them is worth writing.
- **Gives** better marks, an extension, paid reading work, and a name worth something on paper.
- **Says no by** closing the door politely and letting her work stand on its own.

## Jules — she runs the crowd, 21

- **Wants** to be the one everyone arranges themselves around, and to be sure she earned it.
- **Doesn't giggle and doesn't raise her voice.** She watches, she decides, and she's usually
  right — which is what makes her hard to argue with.
- **Found** on the quad between classes, at the union, and at the row on Friday and Saturday.
- **Gives** invitations, the row, and cover from her own friends.
- **She's also the one making Nell small**, so every dare is a humiliation and a way in at the same
  time. *Do this and we'll stop laughing.*
- **Says no by** letting Bree do the talking.
- **In the end she's a target herself.** That's the payoff for everything she put Nell through, and
  it's the only thread in this game between two women.

### Bree and Paige — the two beside her

- **Bree** says the cruel thing out loud. **Paige** is lovely to her face and lets Bree do it.
- **Thin on purpose.** They're the crowd's voice, not two more climbs of their own, and they're in Nate's year —
  which is how what happens on the quad gets home before she does.

## Sam — in her class, and the only one she chooses

- **She's the one who wants.** Every other climb in this game is somebody wanting her. This is the
  one running the other way, and until now she had no want of her own anywhere.
- **Wants** her, the ordinary way. He's her age, he isn't paying for anything, and he asks for nothing
  she can put a price on.
- **Everybody likes him**, and that's the mechanic rather than the flavour: being seen with him is
  worth something, and losing him is visible to people who aren't her.
- **Found** in the classroom in the mornings, and at the union after.
- **Gives** nothing the game can count. He's the ordinary life she's trading away, and the person
  she has to lie to — **the only cost in this game with no money attached to it.**
- **Says no by** believing her. He doesn't check up on her and he doesn't catch her out, which is
  worse than if he did.
- **In the first build he is talking, and that is all.** Him finding out is a later build.

## Cara — her friend, and the only one who wants nothing

- **Wants** her time, which is the one thing Nell is running out of. **Everybody else in this game
  pays her. This is the one person who costs her** — and that's what makes dropping her the cheapest
  hour to free up, and what makes it hurt.
- **Found** at the union tables, in the ten minutes before class, and on the walk to the bus. She's
  also the other end of the phone: **the messages are hers.**
- **Gives** nothing she can spend. What she says out loud — *you look wrecked*, *where were you
  Thursday* — is how rest and clean get said without a number on screen.
- **Says no by** going quiet. She never confronts her and never judges. She just gets harder to
  reach: a missed coffee, a half-answered message, a thread that gets shorter every week. She's the last person
  who still talks to Nell like she's who she was in September.
- **She repeats what the crowd says and never knows it's true.** Finding out is a later build.

## Reyes — the officer

- **Wants** the rules kept. He's strict and he's not for sale.
- **Found** at the station, the park after dark, the strip at closing.
- **Gives** trouble, or the absence of it.
- **He wants nothing from her**, which makes him the only man in the game she can't trade with.

## What this page does not decide

The steps with any of them, or how far each goes · anything in their voice · who finds out what,
and when · what being caught actually costs.

## What it hands to the steps after it

- Nate: the ladder from *he can't stand her* to *he shows it* — the biggest one in the house
- Owen: what the move up actually asks for, step by step
- Hale: what a bad set of marks costs, since nothing yet says
- Reyes: he's a threat with no trigger. Something has to reach him
- Cara: what going quiet actually looks like, message by message — she is a loss with no steps written
- Sam: one conversation a week is not a climb yet. What he asks for, and what she can't tell him
- every one of them needs their hours turned into where they actually stand, room by room
