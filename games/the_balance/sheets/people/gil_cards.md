## Why this page exists

`BASE.md` rule 1: *"Every row has a card. If someone is in a room at an hour, that room has their
card for that hour."* Round 3 gave Gil twelve rows. **Eight of them are dead** — every garage hour
and every bedroom hour. The four that read as covered are all the kitchen, and they are covered by
chore cards, not by him.

**The number that matters: he has a face four hours a week.**

`friday_payment` is his only portrait canvas — the kitchen, Friday, 17:00 to 21:00. Every other hour
of the week he is either a line of prose or nothing at all. Out of 168 hours he is a person for four.

After this page he is findable and awake for **about a hundred and fifteen**, plus the hours he is
asleep in a room she can walk into.

## What he does for money

**He is a mechanic.** *(LO, 2026-09-23.)* He works at a two-bay shop on the strip — eight until
half five, Monday to Friday, never a weekend.

**The garage at home is not the shop.** It holds one old car he has been rebuilding for years. A
project, not upkeep. That is why he is out there every evening and all weekend and it is still not
finished.

**No customers at the house.** *(LO's call, 2026-09-23.)* Nobody else's car is ever in that
driveway. The house stays closed.

**Why the trade matters more than the job title.** A mechanic's wage, and her mum doing three nights
a week on a ward. That is not a comfortable house — and `systems/money.md` says he found **$2,400**
and paid a semester up front and has never once mentioned it. `people/the_cast.md:14` — *"he has
never once mentioned what he paid."* The money now explains itself, and the $150 every Friday stops
reading as meanness. **It is money this house actually needs back.**

**And the shop goes on the map.** *(LO's call, 2026-09-23, over my recommendation — I argued for
keeping it a word and he chose the place. Recorded so the reasoning is visible either way.)*

⚠️ **This does not break `BASE.md:97`.** That rule reads *"If a place has nothing in it yet, nobody
has a row there."* It is conditional. Build the place and he gets a row — the rule is satisfied, not

**Ten blank hours become nine and a half at a bench**, and it is the first place in this game that

## The garage

**Four of his eight dead rows, and the biggest empty block in the house.** On ward nights he is out
there from seven until eleven — four hours, on his own, with her mum at work, Nate on the sofa and
Tasha upstairs.

Five lines across four of your pages put him there and until round 3 he had no garage row at all.
`places/the_house.md:24` — *"Gil, late."* `systems/her_week.md:109` — *"after ten, every night, Gil
still in the garage."*

**Why this is the right card for him specifically.** `systems/the_two_doors.md:47` — ***"Gil never
opens a step. She opens all of them."*** He will never come and get her. So his card has to be a
place she walks to — and the garage is off the street, round the side, past the front door. **There
is no reason to go out there except him.** Walking out is the move, and it costs no meter.

**What the player can do:**

1. **Stand there a while.** Half an hour.
2. **Hand him things.** Half an hour. He does not ask; she works out what he is reaching for.
3. **Leave him to it.**

**And the car is the way in.** He loves it, and nobody ever asks him about it. A man who says
nothing has exactly one subject he will carry himself — **and it is not a step, it is just the
car.** That is the only real conversation in the game he holds up his own end of.

### The garage says the two things he already counts

The game keeps `fridays_missed` and `home_after_ten`, and **both are read only at dinner** —

**The same two bands go on this card.** No new counter, no new flag, no new line of engine — the

**And it lands harder here.** At the table he says it with three other people listening, so he keeps

## The two things that are not presence

### The lift — the one thing he gives

`places/the_house.md:27`, your line, never built: ***"A lift from Gil is a favour."***

**His own week picks the hour.** He leaves at 08:00, Monday to Friday, and that is the only hour he
can drop her. Weekends he never goes out, so there is no weekend lift and no rule needed to say so.

**And it lines up on its own.** Her class is at nine. The bus stop's description already says the
bus *"takes forty minutes to do what a car does in fifteen."* So the lift saves her **$2 and
twenty-five minutes** — the difference between leaving at ten to eight and leaving at eight.

**She buys it with an hour in the garage the evening before.** One flag, clears overnight, same
shape as her own door. **No meter, no counter, no ladder.**

### And the second lift, which the shop gives for free

**Be at the shop before half five and she rides home.** No flag, no favour asked, nothing bought the
night before — he is leaving anyway and she is standing there.

**The pairing that makes it a real plan:** the one o'clock shift ends at four. She has an hour and a
half on that strip with nothing to do and $2 in her pocket for the bus. **Or she walks two hundred
yards and waits in his shop.**

That is the whole shape of him in one choice: the thing he gives costs her nothing and he will never
once offer it.

**Why it matters now.** With the chore money gone, Gil takes $150 a week off her and gives her
nothing whatever. This is the only thing on this page that goes the other way — and because he is a
mechanic, it needs no explaining at all. The car runs because he keeps it running.

### The dinner call — the only thing in the game that moves him

`systems/chores.md:76`, your line: *"**Dinner on a ward night.** If she cooks, there's dinner. If she
doesn't, there's none, and nobody says anything."*

**That is true and it is the problem.** She cooks, half an hour of her time goes, a meal is on the
table, and the game does not notice.

**Who is in this house at seven on a ward night:** Gil in the garage · Nate on the sofa · Tasha in
her room · her mum on the ward. **Calling them is her taking three people out of three rooms**, and
one of them is Gil.

**Every other surface in this game is him already sat somewhere.** This is the one where she moves
him — and it is a plate of food, not a step.

**What it is:** a card in the kitchen, **Monday, Wednesday and Friday, 19:00 to 20:00, only if the
dinner chore is done.** The exact mirror of the `dinner` card that already runs the other four
nights — same room, same hour. Nothing new in the engine.

**One call, not three trips.** She cooks between half five and seven, goes off, comes back at seven,
and the table has people at it because she put them there. Walking to each room is better and it is
a round of its own.

**Nobody refuses.** Refusing is a ladder. And the week varies it for free: **Friday, Tasha is out
from six**, so Monday and Wednesday it is four at the table and Friday it is three.

## Two things that changed on the way in

**1 · Their door is the ROOM, not a shut door.** `walks.py` caught it: her mum's `mum_in_bed` card

NPC, so **both cards draw**. He is now in that room with her, which is what `the_two_doors.md:18`

**2 · The bathroom lock needed four gates, not two.** `entry_conditions` was a flat OR stating when
the door was open, and a second person under OR *opens* the door for the first. The runtime would
have nested a stage helper; **the importer refuses to** — `template_import.py:5634` rejects a nested
`type = "stage"` and `:5643` rejects `logic = "OR"` inside a helper at all. So the disjunction is
split into one AND-only helper per arm and negated at the reader. Four helpers, in
`0_systems_spec.toml`.
