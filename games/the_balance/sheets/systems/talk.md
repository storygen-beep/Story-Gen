## What it is

**How she finds out what's happening, and how she finds out what people say about her.**

Both come from the same act — standing somewhere and listening — so they're one thing, not two.

## Where she hears it

The union tables · the few minutes before class starts · the quad between lectures · the gym
locker room · the row · and her messages, **which are Cara's** — she is the only person who
texts her about anything but work.

**It costs her a few minutes.** Listening is something she chooses to do, not something that
happens at her while she walks past.

## The two kinds

**Where people are.** *The third floor's where people go after eight.* *There's something at the
row on Friday.* This is what turns walking into a dark building into a decision instead of an
accident.

**What people say about her.** Her name in someone else's mouth. **This is how the player finds
out that reputation or exposure moved** — no number, no bar, just two people talking who don't
know she's behind them.

## Three rules that keep it a rumour

1. **It goes stale.** *Tonight* means tonight. If she doesn't go, it's gone, and there's something
   else next week.
2. **It's sometimes wrong.** She turns up and there's nobody there. A rumour that's always true is
   an arrow pointing at content.
3. **It gets sharper as she climbs.** Early, it's about other people. Later it's about her. Later
   still it's specific enough to frighten her — somebody describing a room she recognizes.

## Why it earns its own page

It comes from six rooms and it changes what she does in all the others. Those six rooms are worth
walking through because of it, and that's the whole reason the union and the quad exist.

## What this page does not decide

The lines themselves · how many rumours are live at once · whether anyone can be asked directly,
rather than overheard · what she can do with something she's heard about somebody else.

## What it hands to the steps after it

- every room that produces talk needs lines, and they have to change as she does or they're stale
  by the third visit
- what she hears about herself has to be written at three or four heights, tracking her meters
- somebody eventually has to say something about the stream. That's the loudest thing this can
  ever do, and nothing yet says when
- being able to *use* what she's heard — on the crowd, on Nate, on Tasha — is a whole thing nobody
  has designed
