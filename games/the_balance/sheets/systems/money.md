## What she owes

Gil paid **$2,400** for the semester. She pays him back **$150 every Friday**, so a semester
takes sixteen weeks and then it is clear.

Then the next semester posts, for more, and nobody explains the rise.

**Her mum still doesn't know he paid.** The game remembers whether she knows, and only four things
can change it: Gil telling her, Lynn seeing the bank, Nell saying it herself, or Tasha using it.
Nothing else may quietly flip it.

## The three ways to earn, at the start

| | what it is | what holds it back |
|---|---|---|
| **The cafe** | **a three hour shift, $3** | nothing rations the shifts — the wage is the whole limit |
| **Going live** | the phone, paid per session | she has to be clean, and every session puts more of her in the open |
| ~~Chores~~ | **removed 2026-09-23.** Chores exist, they cost half an hour, they pay nothing | — |

⚠️ **There is no floor under her any more.** Chores used to be it — small on purpose, there to make
the phone a **choice** instead of a wall she got forced through. With chores paying nothing, week
one is **$10 and no way to earn until she has the cafe job**, and the first Friday cannot be paid.
That is a real consequence of removing the chore money and it is not solved on this page.

**Held for later releases:** reading for Hale · Tasha's parties and the bar · the till and the

## Why it's tight at the start and not forever

**Week one she doesn't have the job** — she has to go and ask for it. Once she does, every shift in
the week is open to her and one of them still only pays $3, so the first Fridays genuinely hurt,
and that's what gets her moving. **Putting off the cafe is allowed, and it costs her the first
Friday.**

By about week four the shifts are regular. **Four shifts at $55 is $220**, which covers the payment
with **$70** left. Money stops being the squeeze, and what she wants takes over as the reason.

**The counter doesn't pay more an hour — it pays more shifts.** The rate only rises at the closing
shift, which is **$11 an hour and better tips, about $70**, because it is late and there is nobody
else on the floor to share them with. Four shifts and a closing one is about **$290**.

**The number that changes is her hours, not the payment.** She can pay Gil off entirely with clean
work. She just can't do it and have anything left.

## If she misses a Friday

It rolls over. There's no fail screen and she is never locked out.

The cost is him, not the money: **he asks about it while her mum is in the room.** Miss a second
one and the house rules tighten for a week — in by ten, which shuts the late shift and the late
stream.

## What money is for

It buys **reach** — the bus pass, the ring light, the clothes, the debt itself. Money that only
buys meter points buys nothing.
