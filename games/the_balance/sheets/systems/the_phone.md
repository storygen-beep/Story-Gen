## What's on the phone

**Two things only: messages, and a streaming app.** No bank, no map, no job board, no battery to
worry about.

**Nobody pays her monthly. People follow her, and every stream pays on the night.** Following is
free and it's what makes the next stream worth more.

**She can see how many.** The number sits on the app screen and she watches it move — it's the one
number in the game that only ever goes up.

**It doesn't start at zero.** She's been doing this since the summer, quietly, to a few hundred
people who have never seen her face. That's why one of them already knows her by name.

## Going live

She needs three things: to be **clean**, to be **alone**, and about **an hour**.

**She starts it from the phone, wherever she's standing** — but the app only offers her the places
the scene can actually happen. Somewhere else and it names the room she'd have to be in.

**Her own room, with the door shut**, is the one she has from the start. That's the same door Gil's
rule says stays open when anyone's on the landing, so the stream sits on top of a house rule from
the very first session.

**The toilet at work is the second, and exposure is what opens it.** One room, a lock that works,
and the person who opens it next could be anybody. It matters because it's the only place she can
go live **while she's on a shift** — which is the collision at the bottom of this page, answered.
It's shorter than a session at home, pays the same, and costs more of her.

Later releases open the rest.

**What it pays is never the same twice.** Every session pays somewhere inside a range, so she can
have a good night and a night that wasn't worth getting undressed for.

| followers on screen | a session pays |
|---|---|
| **under 500** | **$8 to $25** |
| **500** | **$20 to $60** |
| **2,000** | **$50 to $120** |

**Followers are what lift the range**, and they go up when she streams, and further up when she does
something she hasn't done before. **The next number is always visible**, so she can see what she's
short of — and what it would take to get there. There's no way to work out in advance what a night clears — which
is the point, because Friday is a fixed number and this isn't.

## What it costs

**Exposure.** Every session puts more of her out where it can be seen, and none of it comes back.

**It only moves on a first time.** A session that is the same as the last one pays money and
followers and moves exposure **by one point.** A session where she does something she has not done
before moves it **by three.** The named steps on the meters page sit fifteen to twenty points apart,
so a run of ordinary nights barely shifts her and a run of new ones changes what she is.

**That is what stops her grinding it.** Exposure measures how much of her is out there, not how
many hours she sat in front of the phone.

**So two things move every time she goes live, and both only go one way.** Followers climb — that's
the reward stacking up. Exposure climbs — that's the chance somebody places her, stacking up too.
The same hour feeds both.

As it goes, people start recognizing her in the street — which is a door opening, not just a
punishment. That's the trade the whole game is built on: the money and the being-looked-at come
from the same place as the risk of being known.

## When she can actually do it

The house is quietest on **weekday afternoons** and after everyone's asleep. Nate is almost always
in, and Tasha comes back at four. So the good hours are exactly the hours the cafe wants too.

## Who's watching

Strangers, mostly, and they come back if she gives them a reason.

**They stay strangers, and that's the state at the first build.** Nobody watching has a name yet,
and nobody she knows is on the other side of it. The day that stops being true is content, and it's
not written.

## How it grows

Regulars return, and returning viewers ask for more than last time. **The asking is the ladder** —
she is never pushed, she's requested, and saying yes is hers.
