## What it is

**Somebody does something small to her, in public.** Two kinds, and they are not the same thing.

## The random kind

**Nobody, no name.** On the quad, in the lecture hall, at the union, at the bus stop. A seat taken
when she goes to sit down. Something said as she walks past. Someone who doesn't move out of the
way. Being talked over by people who didn't notice she was talking.

**Nobody in these needs a name**, which is what makes it cheap enough to be everywhere — the same
reason the walk-ins work.

## It keys off exposure, and that's how exposure is felt

| | what it sounds like |
|---|---|
| **early** | petty and general. She's new and she's nobody, and it isn't really about her |
| **middle** | it's about **her** now. Somebody has decided what she is |
| **late** | **specific.** Someone repeats something they heard, and she knows which night it came from |

**She never sees a number.** She finds out where her exposure is because strangers know things.

## This is *talk*, turned around

`talk.md` is what she **hears without being meant to**. This is the same sentence **said to her face**. A rumour she
caught on Tuesday at the union is what somebody says to her on Thursday on the quad — and pairing
them is what makes both land harder than either would alone.

## What she can do about it is set by where she stands

```text
   take it and say nothing        answer back        somebody answers for her
            |                          |                        |
     nobody knows her          she's known enough       parked with reputation
```

**Low, she takes it**, because answering back with nobody on her side just gives them more. **Higher,
it lands.** Answering back in front of people is the one thing here that **moves where she stands** —
up when it lands, and that's the only thing this writes.

## Jules's version is a different animal

**Hers always ends in a dare.** The random kind never does — it's just the day being worse. That
difference is the whole reason getting in feels like an escape, and it's why the crowd is a way out
of the thing the crowd is doing.

**And doing one turns this page off for a week.** The random kind goes quiet — strangers stop,
nobody says why, and nobody announces it. Then it starts again. **That is what a dare buys in the
first build, and it is the only thing it buys**, because everything else the crowd could give her
is waiting.

## Two rules

1. **It never blocks her.** She still makes the class, the shift and the bus.
2. **It's never violent.** Small, public, and easy for them to say they never did — the kind of
   thing that sounds like nothing when she tries to describe it.
