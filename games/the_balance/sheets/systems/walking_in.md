## What it is

She opens a door, or turns a corner, and there are people in the middle of something.

**At home it's two doors and she knows who's behind them** — Nate's room, and the master bedroom on
the nights her mum is in it. **Those two have their own page**, because the people behind them have
names and that changes everything about how they work.

**On campus it's strangers, and it's everywhere:** the library's third floor after eight, the gym
showers, the department corridor once it empties, a classroom between classes, behind the row
houses, the quad late. **Nobody in these needs a name**, which is what makes it cheap enough to be
everywhere.

⚠️ **In the first build only one of them is open: the classroom between classes.** The rest of that
list is waiting. It has to be one of them, because otherwise every door she walks in on for four
weeks is at home — and the whole point of the game is that college is what changes her. The
classroom costs nothing: the room is already there, and it is empty on a nine to twelve timetable.

## What climbs is her reaction, not her actions

```text
  back out          freeze in the doorway          stay and watch
       |                    |                            |
       +----- low ----------+--------- middle -----------+----- high ----+
                                                                        |
                              let herself be seen · get spoken to · be asked in
```

**Corruption decides what's on the menu. She still picks from it.**

- **Low** — she can back out or freeze. Watching properly isn't on offer. She can't make herself.
- **Middle** — she can stay and watch.
- **High** — she can let herself be seen, be spoken to, and eventually be asked in.

**It climbs itself.** Taking the furthest thing available moves her corruption, which opens the next
thing at the next door. She never grinds a number — she gets further because she went further
last time.

## The two meters do different jobs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Corruption** | how far she'll go |
| **Exposure** | what it costs when somebody sees her doing it |

**So watching is free. Being seen watching is not.**

## Two rules about how it's shown

1. **The first one is written for her.** She walks in, she leaves, and no choice is offered. It teaches
   what this is, and it makes the second one mean something.
2. **Never hide the option she can't take.** She sees that there's something there and that she
   can't do it — one line, not an empty screen. That's how the meter stays readable without ever
   being a number on screen.

## How often

**It gets more likely as she gets further**, because she stops avoiding the places it happens in
without admitting that's what she's doing. The engine already does walk-ins this way, so this costs
no new machinery.
