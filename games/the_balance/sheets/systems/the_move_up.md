## She does not start with the job

**She is not on the schedule when the game begins.** Gil names the opening at the kitchen table —
one line, and he never says it twice. **That puts the debt and the job in the same breath**, which
is him telling her how to pay him without asking for anything.

- **Applying is her first meeting with Owen.** He looks her over, he is exact about the work, and
  he hands out nothing for nothing. His whole character arrives in one short scene.
- **He never turns her down.** If the door can shut, the game can stall on the first day. **The no
  is in the hours, not at the door** — he takes her on and gives her the worst two shifts of the
  week.
- **She can put it off.** The phone is already in her pocket and it pays the same night. Slow and
  honest against fast and seen is the first real choice in the game, and it only exists because
  nobody handed her the job.
- **The cost of putting it off is the first Friday**, which she then can't pay. The game already
  counts the Fridays she missed. It teaches the rule by letting her break it.

## The three steps

Only three, and all three are inside the first build.

| | what it gives her | what it asks for |
|---|---|---|
| **The floor** | two shifts, the worst two. Five hours at $9 and about $10 in tips — **$55 a shift, $110 a week.** Under the payment, on purpose | showing up |
| **The counter** | **four shifts**, same $55, and some say in **which** ones. **$220 a week** — the first week she can pay Friday and still eat. Everyone in the room has to come to her | about eight shifts worked · and he stands closer than he needs to in the office, and she doesn't step back |
| **Closing** | five shifts, one of them five to ten at **$11 an hour with better tips — about $70.** The building is him and her | about twenty shifts worked · and the office door shut, and more than his hands |

**Notice what the counter actually pays in: shifts, not a rate.** The hourly only rises at closing,
and only because it is late and there is nobody else on the floor to share the tips with. That is
the rule at the top of this page doing its job.

**What closing really costs is the ten o'clock.** She is home after Gil's rule, every time, and
that is the price the pay is hiding.

## Both keys, every step, and it needs both

- **Shifts worked** is proof she can do the job.
- **How far she's gone with him** is why he'd pick her over somebody else.
- **Shifts alone can never reach closing.** She can work every hour he gives her and stay on the
  counter — the work proves she's able, and it never answers why he'd be alone in a locked building
  with her at ten.
- **And the other way round is worse for her.** Going further with him while the work is sloppy
  moves her nowhere, because he's a strict boss before he is anything else. That is what stops the
  cafe turning into a menu.

## She can't slide backwards

**Shifts worked never drops.** What he does instead is **hold her where she is** — she meets the
step and nothing happens, and the schedule goes up on the wall the same as always.

**That is the only punishment in the cafe, and he never announces it.** He doesn't threaten, he
doesn't argue, and he doesn't explain. The schedule is how he talks.

## Above closing

**Keys and opening the place alone are cut.** They climb into being trusted with a building, which
is a promotion in a job and not a step in this game. The cafe opens and shuts on its own hours like
every other room.

**What waits above closing instead: she writes her own schedule.** She stops being given shifts and
starts choosing them. In a game where the squeeze is hours — nine o'clock class, Friday's payment,
home by ten — **letting her pick her own hours is the most valuable thing the cafe can ever hand
her.** A later build. Named here so it isn't invented twice.

## Where the first build ends

**The schedule with her name not on the closing shift.** It reads the same as the first day, when
she wasn't on it at all — the same board, the same feeling, four weeks apart.
