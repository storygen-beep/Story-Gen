## The job

Waitress — **and she doesn't have the job when the game starts.** There's an opening, Gil names it
at the kitchen table, and she has to go and ask for it. Applying is her first meeting with Owen.

A shift is about five hours, and **Owen decides how many she gets.** He's a strict boss: exact about
the work, quick to notice when it's sloppy, and he hands out nothing for nothing.

Her first week is two shifts, the worst two — around **$110** with tips, which doesn't cover the
$150. By about week four she's on four or five, and a clean week is the **$220** the money page
promised.

Her classes decide which shifts she can take, so the cafe and college are always pulling at each
other.

## Moving up

**Applying → the floor → the counter → the closing shift.** Three steps and all three are in the
first build. **Her own keys and opening the place alone are cut** — they climb into being trusted
with a building, and the cafe opens and shuts on its own hours like every other room.

**The closing shift is the door the first release ends on.** She can see the schedule with her name
not on it, and closing is the shift where the cafe is him and her.

Each step buys **hours and access**, plus a little more an hour. It is never just a raise.

**Two keys open every step, and it needs both:** shifts actually worked, and how far she's gone with
Owen. Work alone moves her slowly. The adult side alone moves her nowhere — otherwise the
waitress work is pointless and the cafe turns into a menu.

**The steps, the amounts and what he asks at each one are on the move up page.**

## The customers

The other ladder, and it's the opposite shape: lots of people, shallow each, cash in hand.

**It climbs:** a hand that doesn't get moved · showing more · the toilet, for money.

**Tips are how they ask.** A bigger tip is the request, and taking it is the answer — roughly **$10**
for letting something happen at the table, **$30** for more, **$80** in the toilet. Faster than any
shift, which is exactly the danger.

**So it has brakes.** Only some customers ask. Only when the room allows it. Every time, it adds to her
exposure — and the toilet has a door anyone can open.

**The customers stay faceless.** They're strangers with money, not people with names — that's what
makes the tips ladder usable at all.

## The till

Money in reverse, and the game remembers. It's always there, it's never free, and taking from it is
the one thing Owen can't be persuaded out of.
