## 1. Who she is

**Nell Vasey, 19.** She was supposed to leave in September. The money wasn't there, so she
didn't, and the gap year is quietly becoming her life. She has never lived anywhere else. Her
room is the room she was eight in.

There is one bathroom for five adults. Her father takes board off everyone in the house —
**£50 a week, Sunday night, cash, at the kitchen table** — because everyone contributes, and
he says it the same way every time. Her Saturday shift at the shop on the parade pays £36.
The gap is covered by the allowance, and the allowance has to be asked for, out loud, itemised,
in front of whoever is in the kitchen.

**What she has to lose:** nothing dramatic — and that is the point. She has no story about
herself yet. The only number anyone has ever put on Nell Vasey is the one on the back of her
father's envelope, and everyone in the house has seen it.

## 2. The appetite that never fills

**To be wanted by the people who set her price — and to keep the number moving.**

It cannot complete. There is no figure that settles it; the moment one is agreed she wants the
next one set by someone else. No villain, no door at the end.

**What does release 41 add?** A new relative in the box room, a new thing in the house that
turns out to have a price, or another rung on someone who already sits at that table. The
household is a machine for putting numbers on her.

## 3. What she is becoming — as ACCESS

**Bottom.** She asks. She itemises. She waits for the bathroom and she knocks on every door in
the house including the one to the room she sleeps in, because the catch is loud and her
mother is asleep in the afternoons. She is the last person to be handed anything.

**Top.** **She names the number and the table pays it.** The Sunday envelope runs the other
way. There is no room in the house she has to be invited into, no door she waits outside, and
nothing she wants that she has to justify. The house has reorganised its money and its hours
around her, and nobody in it has ever said out loud what happened.

### The ascent tiers

| tier | what going further means | 15 | 35 | 55 | 75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`nerve`** | how far she'll go *with blood* | stops managing herself around them | lets it happen and doesn't move | initiates, undeniably | it's the arrangement, and the house runs on it |

| **`price`** | what she'll take money for, and how openly | asks for more than she needs | takes a thing she'd have refused | states a figure to someone's face | sets the board and the allowance both |
| **`appetite`** | what she wants for its own sake, with nothing on the table | notices she's been waiting for it | goes looking | asks for it with no cover story | **the game stops offering and starts firing** |

⚠️ **`appetite` is a ratchet, not arousal.** Arousal is volatile layer-2 weather. `appetite` is
the standing baseline of what she needs before anything happens that day.

**Counterweight: `standing`** — starts 100. What the house thinks Nell Vasey is. Every ask,
every itemised line, every time she takes something spends it. It buys nothing. It is what she
is paying with.

## 4. The charge

**Reversal**, primarily; **taboo** underneath, and it is blood the whole way — father, brother,
uncle, mother. No step, no patch, no asterisk.

The reversal is specific and it is the reason this is a game and not a scene: **the kitchen
table is a pricing mechanism.** It exists to decide what Nell is owed and what Nell owes, once a
week, in public, in front of her family. The ascent is her learning to move other things across
that same table until the direction of the money reverses and the people who audited her are
justifying themselves to her.

## 6. Register

- **`narration_person` = `second`.** Declared once, immutable after v0.1 ships.
- **Where the crude register lives: the standing surfaces.** The bathroom, the landing between
  two doors, the kitchen after Denise has gone to work, the flat roof outside two windows, her
  own room in the afternoon with her mother asleep down the hall. Daily, re-enterable, and
  crude. The once-only beats can be careful; **the surfaces she returns to fifty times carry
  the crudest writing in the game.**
