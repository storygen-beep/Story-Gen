## The rule this scene exists to obey

**R5e — a gate opens when she has earned it; an OFFER is there before she needs it.**

> **Test: can she take this when she doesn't need to?** ✅ **Yes.** No condition on `money`.

⚠️ **This is the shape two games got wrong with no shared context between them.** Both wired the
same offer behind a *lack* — `money lt 260` in one, `prep lt 30` in the other — and one of them
earns $208 in a day against a $260 week, making its best scene close to unreachable. Neither author
was careless: the doctrine covered the choice she is *refused* and the choice she *declines*, and had
nothing for a choice offered **before she needs it**.

**The field check:** three of the four corpus games with an obligation let her volunteer for a
bigger one, and **not one gates it on scarcity.** The strongest gates on *that she knows* —
`$robindebtknown is 1` — never on being short.

⚠️ **The narrow claim, not the broad one.** *"Never gate a grant on a lack"* is false — a rescue
should require needing rescue, and 50 sites across our games do it correctly. What holds is: **if
taking on a burden is what opens a person, it must be reachable while she is fine.**
