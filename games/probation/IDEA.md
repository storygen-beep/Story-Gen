## 1. The fantasy — what the player comes here to feel

**Which shape?** **Mix: rise by want, run on an order.** She picked nothing about the sentence, but
everything she does with it is her own want: the goal, a rival, a clock. The clock is Tuesday at
two, every week. (Nothing in the skill maps an `order` hold to a shape. Finding 1.)

**In one sentence:** *Every hour of her life is logged, and she finds out that being watched turns
her on, and that every man holding her paperwork can be made to lie for her.*

**The model to beat:** **Shady Deals**. It is a female-lead crime-world game, and players praise it
for its goal (*"The goal is to become rich"*). **What ours does better:** a goal with a date
the player can count down to, a mystery that pays out on a schedule, and one face kept for the
whole game. Its players fault it for all three: *"unnecessary sounds"* instead of *"continuing
the story"*, and *"ZERO actress consistency"* (`the-want.md:40-43`, `:384-385`).

**Which moments does this game promise?**

- [x] **being seen** — the main one. The box logs a point every sixty seconds, and Delgado reads
      the page out loud.
- [x] **her body as the price for something she needs** — a signature, a fault report, an hour
      the log does not show.
- [x] **a consequence she has to live with** — every Tuesday, the log is read back to her.
- [x] **her firsts** — first hour past eight, first time over the bridge, first dot that lies.
- [ ] taboo at home — no. Her room over the laundry is a home, but nobody in it is family.

## 2. The promise — what keeps pulling the player forward

- **The goal:** **the box comes off** **by the day-90 review.** Delgado says it at intake: ninety
  days, a full sheet every Friday, a clean log, and he takes it off her leg himself. That puts the
  goal and the appetite in a fight. The review wants a clean log; she wants the far side of the
  bridge. So the game is about making a dirty life **look** clean, and that is what the `vouch`
  meter already is: people lying for her.
- **The mystery:** **a dot on her log she never made.** On her second Tuesday the report shows the
  box at the yards across the river, 02:10 Saturday, while she was asleep upstairs. Delgado calls
  it "a signal fault. Probably." **The box can lie, and somebody has already made it lie.** It pays
  out in three parts: Tobin's step 1 (0.1) shows boxes "get helped"; around 0.3 she learns whose
  dot it was; around 0.5 she learns who does the helping and what they charge.
- **The rival:** **Delgado.** He does not want her, and he does not want her to fail. He wants the
  log to be true. He is the only person between her and day 90, and he is not corrupt.

The guidance page keeps two cards up from the first screen: *Day 90 — N days left* and *The dot at
the yards*. (Needs a counter for days. The engine check is a spine question for SP1, not assumed here.)

## 3. The people who carry it

**The companion — NEW, if LO wants her: Nell, 31.** She finished her own probation last year and
works the diner counter nights. She is one step ahead: off the box, and already been across the
bridge. *"Foil does nothing. Don't bother. Here's what works."* She fills the old design's diner
counter slot. **Fallback without a fifth person:** Rae. But Rae is written as someone who never
asks, and a companion's job is to push.

**The pressure-man:** **Tobin.** He services the boxes and can make one say anything. His demand
drives her choices release after release: each thing he does to the box has a price, and he names
it. **The price of her no:** he writes the report straight, and Delgado reads it at two. **Her way
out:** she can ask Delgado for a different technician. It is free, and it costs her the one man who
can fake a dot.

**Her face:** one performer, kept for the whole game. Dark hair grown out from a short cut, a thin
build, and a small tattoo on the inside of one wrist. **The black band on her left ankle appears in
every still where we can get it.** That is her silhouette. Built later with `[player_portrait]`.

### Why A

- **Tobin's step proves the whole promise in one scene.** It has the box, the log, the pressure,
  the mystery's first clue, and Delgado reading it two hours later. The player learns the game's
  engine: *her body buys changes to the record.*
- **B is the strongest arc,** and Marty is still the man the player would miss. But step 1 is
  gentle. It is the best second relationship to open, not the best first impression.
- **C is the hottest first moment** and the most original. It only works if the laundry already
  feels like home, so it opens better in week two.

You can also take a mix: A's mystery and dot with C's scene. The dot at the yards was logged the
same night she sat in the towel.
