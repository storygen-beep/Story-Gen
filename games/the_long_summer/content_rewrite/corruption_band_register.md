# Corruption-Band Register — Maya's Four Narrators

> **Mandatory read before writing any band-gated variant or any high-corruption scene.**
> Source: `final_book.md:450-471` + Prologue exemplar. The corruption stat is the game's central narrative axis. Its prose register is the game's central craft commitment.

## The four narrators are four different writers

The game's strongest design commitment is **distributed self-recognition** — the player feels Maya change through accumulating prose-register shifts, not through an awakening scene. For this to work, the four bands must read as four different narrators who happen to be narrating the same person.

**Common failure (current TLS pattern):** swap a verb, change one adjective, call it a band. *"can't hold eye / holds it / tilts chin / doesn't need mirror"* — same narrator, four lines.

**Target:** each band has a different sentence rhythm, different stance toward Maya's body, different verbs of agency, different relationship to detail.

## The four bands defined

### Closed (corruption 0-24) — *Observing*

**Stance:** Catalogs without naming. Notices being looked at as a *surprise*. Catches herself noticing and is uncertain whether to trust the noticing.

**Sentence rhythm:** Long, hedged, observational. Lots of subordinate clauses with *which* and *because* and *the way*. Few imperatives. Many *seemed*, *almost*, *as if*.

**Verbs:** Passive constructions ("she was watched"), perception verbs ("she felt," "she registered"), with hedging ("she *thought* she felt").

**Pronoun habit:** "she" + adjective + qualifier. *"She was uncertain whether..."*

**Detail relationship:** External. Notices the room more than her body. Uses generic descriptors for things she's claiming for the first time — *Frank's house*, not *my house*; *the kitchen*, not *the kitchen at this hour*.

**Specimen — Prologue N1, Closed-band Maya:**
> "She had never told him she did this — lay in bed mapping the room before she opened her eyes. It felt like something she shouldn't describe out loud, the way some private rituals felt more hers the less they were said."

**Specimen — Closed-band Mirror:**
> She kept her eyes on her hair at the temple and the collar of her shirt and the small cluster of moles at the side of her neck. She did not look back at the part of her face that would look back. She had never been the kind of girl who could hold her own eye in a mirror and the bathroom at Frank's house had not changed that yet.

### Opening (corruption 25-49) — *Attending*

**Stance:** Patterns visible. She catalogs *and names a few of them*. Catches herself noticing her own noticing — and lets it happen anyway.

**Sentence rhythm:** Lengthens further; she has time to think because she's no longer evaluating *whether* to think. The hedges drop one at a time.

**Verbs:** Active perception ("she watched," "she counted"), with self-acknowledgment ("she let herself," "she registered").

**Pronoun habit:** "she" + active verb. *"She watched him watch her."* Acknowledgment of pattern.

**Detail relationship:** Half-internal. Starts to claim specific rooms — *Frank's kitchen at dinner. Frank's office when he's working late.*

**Specimen — Prologue N3 pivot to Opening-tier Maya:**
> "She closed it quietly. She did not slam it. The part of her that slammed doors had not been in the room with her, apparently, for some time — she had left that part of herself somewhere, maybe on Sunday morning, maybe earlier, and what was in her body in the third-floor hallway was the other part, the part that closed doors quietly, the part that would turn out to be most of her from here on out."

**Specimen — Opening-band Mirror:**
> She held her own eye for one second, then two, then four. She catalogued. The jaw was hers — the line her mother had given her, that she had thought about cutting bangs to hide once and then hadn't. The eyes were hers, the same eyes she had spent eighteen years looking out of, slightly darker since the move because she was not sleeping the same. The rest of the face she let stay negotiable. The face was a thing she was learning, and she had not finished.

### Operating (corruption 50-74) — *Deliberate*

**Stance:** Picks her targets. Uses her effect on purpose. Names what she's doing. Still has the artist's honesty — she can name what she's doing *to herself*.

**Sentence rhythm:** Shorter, more declarative. Fewer subordinate clauses. The verbs do more work; the qualifiers do less. Short sentences land beats.

**Verbs:** Active, transitive, of agency. *"She wore. She picked. She tilted. She closed."*

**Pronoun habit:** Possessive surfaces. *"My Thursday. My booth. My shift."*

**Detail relationship:** Internal-instrumental. The room is something she works *with*. The body is something she chooses what to do with.

**Specimen — Operating target:**
> Thursday was the trucker shift and she wore the blue, because the blue ran two dollars more per table than the grey, and she wasn't lying to herself about why anymore. The blue was the one she had bought in town two weeks ago. She had bought it to know what it would be like to spend ten dollars on a shirt that was about the tip jar.

**Specimen — Operating-band Mirror:**
> She tilted her chin. The face was a tool she had been learning the use of, and she had a pretty good handle on it now. The eye contact was a thing she could hold or not hold depending. The smile was a thing she could deploy at three different volumes. She knew which one paid better at the diner and which one paid better with Ryan and which one she was still figuring out for Frank.

### Saturated (corruption 75-100) — *Operating+*

**Stance:** Speaks the language she made. Doesn't need the mirror. *Millhaven* is something she belongs to. Art is the one register the corruption prose still doesn't touch.

**Sentence rhythm:** Minimal hedging. Subject + active verb, often. Declarative cadence — she's no longer narrating decisions, she's reporting outcomes.

**Verbs:** Past-tense routine, present-tense decision. *"She closed the till at eleven. The key went into her pocket."*

**Pronoun habit:** Often drops the subject pronoun in favor of the action. *"Closed the till. Walked the hour home."*

**Detail relationship:** Inhabited-routine. The room and the body are no longer separately notable; they are part of what she does on a Thursday.

**Specimen — Saturated target:**
> She closed the till at eleven. The key went into her pocket. She walked the hour home by the light of other people's porches and thought about nothing, which was the thing she'd been practicing.

**Specimen — Saturated-band Mirror:**
> She did not look in the mirror. The mirror had stopped being a thing she consulted some weeks ago. She brushed her teeth, she rinsed, she pulled the elastic out of her hair. The face she had at twenty-eight or thirty would be different from the face she had now and she was going to be ready for both. The bathroom at Frank's house was the bathroom at Frank's house. The light was harsh; she did not look up.

## Cross-band tells (when registering the same surface event)

**Event: a customer holds her gaze at the diner.**

| Band | Maya's narration |
|---|---|
| **Closed** | "He was looking at her. She did not know what to do with the look. She lifted the coffee pot with both hands because one had started to feel like it might shake." |
| **Opening** | "He was looking. She let him look. She held the coffee pot in one hand because she didn't need the other, and the not-needing was the part she was getting used to." |
| **Operating** | "She let him hold it. She knew the count past which holding meant something. She refilled his cup at the count where it would pay." |
| **Saturated** | "She refilled his cup. She did not break the look. He left a fifteen on a six-dollar check. She wiped the table." |

Same physical event. Four different narrators.

## How to apply in TOML variant blocks

When a canvas has band-gated variant `group` blocks (see `activity_mirror_look` for the showcase), each variant should have:

1. A different opening — not just a different first noun
2. A different sentence-length rhythm
3. A different stance toward the body or the room
4. A different verb register (perception → action → routine)

## Failure mode to avoid

The current `activity_mirror_look` (3_activities.toml:415-435) demonstrates the exact failure to avoid:

```
Closed:    She can't hold her own eye for five seconds.
Opening:   She holds it. Catalogs what she sees.
Operating: She tilts her chin. Tests the angle. She knows what it does.
Saturated: She doesn't need the mirror to know.
```

These are four conditions of one narrator. Same syntax structure (subject-verb), same length, same stance. The change is *what she does with the mirror*, not *who is narrating her*.

The `mirror_look` rewrite in Session 1 sets the template for fixing this everywhere.
