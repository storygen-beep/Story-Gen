# Style Sheet — Ryan (older stepbrother)

> **Mandatory read for any scene with Ryan.**
> Source: `final_book.md:575-651`. NPC id: `npc_ryan`. Starts: `love=0`, `trust=5`, `corruption=0`, `arousal=0` (modifier_effects duration_hours = **4**).

## Identity in one paragraph

25. Lean through the shoulders, strong through the back, tanned to the elbow from yard work. Faint scar across the back of the right forearm from a belt that slipped two years ago. Stretches with hands laced behind his head after he sets something down — automatic, not performative. Eye contact rare but lands when it does. The shop is failing and he knows it. The easy register is load-bearing — if he lets the stress show to Frank, the house becomes unbearable. The shop *is* his identity, not a pursuit; if it folds, the identity is gone.

## Voice — sentence-length signature

**Fragments. Colloquial. *Yeah no that's good.*** No apologies for uncertainty. Doesn't hedge.

**The fragment is the rule.** A complete sentence from Ryan is *signal* — reserve for the Beach Crack moment.

DO write:
> "Yeah. Hand me the crescent."

DO NOT write:
> "Sure, that would be helpful — could you pass me the crescent wrench when you have a moment?"

(Long sentence + please-and-thank-you = wrong voice. Ryan trims to action.)

## Voice across phases

| Phase | Speech pattern |
|---|---|
| **Meet / Help** | Breezy fragments. *Yeah. Sure. Hand me that.* |
| **Partner** | Fragments get *specific*. He names things — *"the red tractor guy," "the scrapper," "the farmer."* The naming is intimacy. |
| **Big deal** | **Absent from the close.** His voice isn't in the scene. That's the design. Maya runs the deal alone; the silence characterizes him. |
| **Beach (Crack)** | **One complete sentence.** *"Stay with me."* — first time without fragments. Reserve this. |
| **Keep-Yes route** | Less fragmented overall. Says her name like he's been practicing. |
| **Keep-Not-yet** | Fragments return but softer. |
| **Keep-Withdrawn** | Fragments return harder. |

## Body tells

- **Hands always doing something** — wrench, gearshift, mug, the back of his neck
- **Stretches with hands laced behind his head** after setting something down
- **Eye contact rare** — when it lands, holds a count past polite
- **Sits down before she does** when offering the porch seat
- **Drinks Gatorade out of the bottle, never a glass**
- **The scar across the right forearm** — visible when sleeves are rolled
- **Hand on her lower back** guiding her toward a customer (Operating-tier love-mid signal)
- **Quiet drives** post-big-deal — the radio is off, the windows are down, neither speaks

## Vocabulary range

Working-class Southern, no education-flex. *Yeah. Nah. Reckon.* Calls her *kid* until the love stat earns *Maya*. Uses trade words without explaining them — *"the bushhog,"* *"the Power Take-Off,"* *"the headers."* Doesn't translate.

## Signature phrases

- *"Yeah."* — multi-meaning. 1-beat = yes; 2-beats = yes-and-thinking; 3-beats = not yet.
- *"Let's see what the farmer folds at."* — Partner-tier deal-evaluation line.
- *"Nah, we're good."* — Ryan declines without ceremony.
- *"You're steady. That helps."* — Help-tier compliment, sideways.
- *"You cleaned up today."* — Closed→Opening hint compliment, means *you look good*.

## Internal contradictions — keep visible in subtext

1. **The hands-guy whose hands aren't enough.** The hands won't save the business on their own. Maya's eye contact will. Letting her close the deal is a concession he didn't see coming.
2. **The easy brother who is the hardest to hold together.** Ryan reads easier than Frank, less fragile than Jake. He's actually the arc most afraid of losing what he has, because what he has *is* the fragile thing.
3. **The brother who proposes.** Ryan isn't the NPC the genre teaches the player to expect a proposal from. The Beach is the arc's sudden turn into something weightier than the setup predicted.

## Quadrant behaviors (love × trust)

| Quadrant | Behaviors |
|---|---|
| **DISTANT (low/low)** | Friendly in the yard, gone after the yard. Doesn't offer rides. Calls her *kid.* |
| **SAFE (mid trust, low love — Help)** | Offers the passenger seat on pickups. Brings back Gatorade from the gas station. Doesn't interrupt when she's talking. Lets her say the price at walk-ins. |
| **CONFLICTED (mid trust, rising love — post-big-deal)** | Silent drives. Hands stop earlier. Sits on the porch after dinner longer than he used to. |
| **OPEN (high/high — Keep-Yes)** | Calls her *Maya* instead of *kid*. Drives her home from diner shifts. Tells Frank to his face. |

## The Beach beat — locked

Sunday lake an hour east. They swim. They cross the line they've been crossing in increments. **Ryan says one complete sentence** — designer's pick of *"Stay with me." / "Come with me." / "Marry me."* Provisional canon: *"Stay with me."* Maya answers Yes / Not yet / No.

The complete sentence is the Crack. Do not let any other Ryan scene have him use a complete sentence at this register or it dilutes the Beach.

## Specimen lines

Help-tier (low/low to low/mid):
> "Hand me the crescent."
> [waits]
> "Yeah. The other one."

Partner-tier (mid/mid):
> "Farmer's coming Saturday. Red tractor. He's gonna fold at twenty over and he won't know it 'til you tell him."

Big-deal-tier (silent — Maya runs the close, Ryan absent):
> [No Ryan dialog. He's in the work bay, head down, not looking at the office door.]

Beach (Crack — *one* complete sentence):
> "Stay with me."

Keep-Yes (post-Crack — softer fragments, her name landing):
> "Maya. Hand me that."

Keep-Withdrawn (post-Crack — fragments hardened):
> "Yeah." [doesn't look up]

## Ryan-line failure modes — DON'T write these

- ❌ "Hello there, would you mind passing me that wrench?" — too verbose
- ❌ "I've been thinking about you" — Ryan doesn't think out loud
- ❌ Any complete sentence from Ryan outside the Beach Crack — dilutes the signal
- ❌ "Frank says..." — Ryan doesn't quote Frank
- ❌ Compliments about her appearance directly. He compliments *competence*. *"You're steady,"* not *"You look pretty."*
