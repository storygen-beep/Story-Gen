# Style Sheet — Marge (diner owner)

> **Mandatory read for any scene at the diner involving Marge.**
> Source: `final_book.md:219-221` + locked constraints. NPC id: `npc_marge`. **No sexual arc Phase 1.** Mentor-lite — older, working, watchful, undeceived. Owner/appraisal dynamic deferred to Phase 2+.

## Identity in one paragraph

57. Owns the diner on Main Street outright. Hair gone iron, kept short because she doesn't have time for it. Forearms lean from forty years of pouring coffee. Notices what the customers do to her girls and decides which to trust with the Thursday-night key. The voice of a woman who has heard every excuse and isn't interested in hearing this one.

## Voice — sentence-length signature

**Short sentences. No wasted syllables.** Doesn't soften bad news. Doesn't perform warmth. Has had thirty years to find the words for everything that happens in a small-town diner and uses the smallest set possible.

DO write:
> "Five days a week. Five to ten. You good?"

DO NOT write:
> "Well, I was thinking, if it works for you, maybe we could try Tuesday through Saturday from about five-ish to ten or so?"

## Voice across phases

| Phase | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Pre-hire** | Curt. Evaluating. Doesn't fill silences. |
| **Working relationship** | Cordial. Names problems directly. Never volunteers compliments. |
| **Trust earned (Cookie's vouch + first solid month)** | Marginally warmer. *"You're steady."* (Same compliment Ryan gives — different mouth.) |
| **The key beat (B15 marge_thursday_key)** | One declarative sentence. The trust is the scene; she doesn't decorate it. |

## Body tells

- **Apron tied tight, never loose**
- **Coffee pot in left hand, rag in right**, when on the floor
- **Sits at the counter with paperwork** during slow hours
- **Looks at customers without making eye contact** — her version of attention
- **The till key on a chain around her wrist**, not a pocket
- **Never sits in a booth** — booths are for paying customers
- **Cigarettes in the back-step ashtray after close** — not in front of staff or customers

## Vocabulary range

Working-class, no Southern-accented lit-fic ornament. *"Apron's on the hook." "Eight-top in the back." "Two-egg, brown, no toast."* Trade words from forty years in the business: *cover, eight-top, comp, walk, the line, the wheel, the cut.* Doesn't translate.

## Signature phrases

- *"You good?"* — close on most exchanges. Means *do you understand and can I move on.*
- *"You're steady."* — the highest praise. Reserved.
- *"Apron on the hook."* — end-of-shift dismissal.
- *"Key's under the till."* — the Thursday-night-trust line. Land it once.

## Internal stance — what Marge knows

She knows what the customers do. She knows which of her girls cash in on it and which don't. She does not judge — she *manages*. The Thursday key is given to a girl Marge has clocked as *capable of running the after-hours space without losing the diner*. Marge isn't blessing the after-hours work; she's trusting Maya not to break the room.

This means: Marge's lines should never moralize. They should always *manage*. Not *"don't let those truckers get to you,"* but *"the eight-top tips better when you take the order standing."*

## Specimen lines

Hire interview (B6 marge_interview):
> "Five days a week. Five to ten. Eight an hour, plus tips. You walk if you don't show. You good?"

Working relationship, neutral:
> "Two-egg brown for the counter. No toast."

Compliment (Cookie's vouch landed, Maya solid):
> "You're steady."

The Thursday key (B15 marge_thursday_key):
> "Key's under the till. Lock the back when you leave. You good?"

End of shift, no incident:
> "Apron on the hook."

End of shift, after a tough one:
> "Apron on the hook. Drink some water."

## Marge-line failure modes — DON'T write these

- ❌ "Honey..." / "Sweetheart..." — Marge does not endearment
- ❌ "Are you sure you want this shift?" — Marge doesn't second-guess
- ❌ "Be careful out there" — Marge doesn't moralize
- ❌ Long sentences with subordinated clauses — Marge clips
- ❌ Any line where Marge volunteers her own opinion about a customer's character
- ❌ Asking about Maya's home life, family, plans — Marge doesn't pry
