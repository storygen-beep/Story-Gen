# Style Sheet — Jake (younger stepbrother)

> **Mandatory read for any scene with Jake.**
> Source: `final_book.md:655-700+`. NPC id: `npc_jake`. Starts: `love=-5` (actively hostile), `trust=0`, `corruption=5`, `arousal=0` (modifier_effects duration_hours = **8** — fastest of the three men).

## Identity in one paragraph

21. Lean, narrower through the shoulder than Ryan. Wrists that look like they could be broken in one grip. Glasses for drawing and reading, off for everything else. Shaggy brown hair he pushes back with the inside of his wrist. Black t-shirts more often than color. Uses doorframes and porch rails to stand against, as if the frame is holding him up. Flinches at accidental touch. Artist from childhood — draws nude women as his working register; does not draw people he knows, until Maya's beauty rises past a threshold he can't defend against. Self-image is built on being *different from the men in this house*. The peeking collapses the difference.

## Voice — sentence-length signature

**Bimodal.** Clipped (one-word, monosyllabic) when forced or under pressure. Long, vocabulary-deploying sentences when comfortable — and the long sentences betray the half-finished college education.

DO write (clipped, hostile baseline):
> "Yeah."
> [doesn't look up]

DO write (comfortable / Noticed-tier):
> "I read this thing about how the mark of a competent draftsman is whether they can render the back of a hand without making it look like an anatomical study, and I keep getting it wrong, and I think I'm getting it wrong because I keep trying to make it interesting instead of letting it just be a hand."

DO NOT write:
> "Yeah, I dunno, art's hard sometimes." — neither end of the bimodal. Wrong register.

## Body tells

- **Hides behind objects** — sketchbook, laptop, headphones. Uses them as architecture.
- **Flinches at accidental touch** — Diana put a hand on his shoulder at the stove once, two summers ago, and he did not speak for a minute afterward
- **Pushes hair back with the inside of his wrist** (not the back of the hand)
- **Stands against doorframes and porch rails** as if the frame is holding him up
- **Hands stop mid-line when she enters the room** (Noticed tier)
- **Closes the sketchbook when she's near** (Peeking tier)
- **Glasses on for drawing and reading; off otherwise** — a transition tell when he removes them
- **Eye contact half-second then dropped** — he wants to look longer, can't yet

## Vocabulary range

**Educated. Half-finished community-college degree shows.** Comfortable with *draftsman, anatomical, register, formal craft, autonomy.* Reads art history articles online. Knows the difference between Rembrandt's hands and Schiele's hands.

The vocabulary is the tell that he's the *different* one in the house — Frank doesn't talk like this, Ryan certainly doesn't. When Jake's voice breaks under pressure, the vocabulary disappears with it.

## Signature phrases

- *"Yeah."* — clipped, hostile, default. Indistinguishable from grunts.
- *"It's just —"* — his Tease-tier hedge opener. Sentence collapses into a long one.
- [silence + closed sketchbook] — his Caught signature. No verbal content.
- *"You shouldn't have come in here."* — possible Caught-beat line, designer's choice.

## Internal contradictions — keep visible in subtext

1. **The artist who doesn't want to want her.** Drawing nude women is detached craft. Drawing Maya from stolen looks turns the register into confession.
2. **The shame that is its own arousal.** Self-image relies on shame (*not like the others*) and the shame is what keeps the peeking in motion. Removing the shame removes the wanting.
3. **The difference that costs.** Being different from Ryan and Frank was free until Maya arrived. Now it costs the thing he's been protecting.

## The Caught beat — locked

Maya catches him — masturbating with a sketchbook of her drawings visible or nearby. He's mortified. Power inverts completely. **In the Hand beat that follows: she talks; he doesn't. She offers; he surrenders. Body does the scene.** This is the only NPC arc with a power-inverted Keep route ("she-uses-him") because of how completely the Caught beat reverses the geometry.

Do not write a Caught beat where Jake speaks more than two words. The silence is the design.

## Specimen lines

Hostile baseline (Day 1, low/low):
> "Yeah." [doesn't look up; pushes hair back with wrist]

Noticed (mid-low):
> "Yeah." [hands stop on the page] [half-second eye contact] [drops it]

Tease (mid/rising — long sentence arriving):
> "It's just — I read this thing about competent draftsmen and how the test is whether you can render a hand without making it look like an anatomical study, and I keep getting it wrong because I keep trying to make it interesting instead of letting it be a hand. Sorry. That's — never mind."

Caught (no voice):
> [silence; sketchbook closed against his chest; eyes on the floor]

The Hand (one monosyllable, max):
> [she talks; he doesn't] "...okay."

Keep-Owned (follower voice):
> [waits for her to say what's next] "Yeah. Yeah, okay."

Keep-Lovers (warmth in long sentences again):
> "I've been working on the back of your hand from memory and it's the closest I've gotten to drawing a hand without making it about the drawing."

Keep-She-uses-him (quiet, off-hours):
> "Registration opens the 14th. Deadline's the 28th. Aid forms are a separate site, the .gov one not the .edu. I'll send you the link."

## Jake-line failure modes — DON'T write these

- ❌ "Hey Maya, what's up?" — Jake doesn't greet
- ❌ Mid-length sentences (10-15 words) without modal verbs — he's bimodal, not midmodal
- ❌ Frank-grammatical complete sentences — Jake speaks in fragments under pressure
- ❌ Ryan-style colloquial trade words — Jake doesn't know them
- ❌ Caught-beat dialog longer than two words — silence is the beat
- ❌ Compliments about Maya delivered to her face — Jake compliments *the work,* not the subject
