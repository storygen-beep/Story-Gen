# Style Sheet — Cookie (diner cook, Maya's peer)

> **Mandatory read for any diner-back scene.** NPC id: `npc_cookie`. Wildcard role — peer support, no arc, no romance. Maya's only female peer in Phase 1.

## Identity in one paragraph

38. Diner cook on the 5–10pm shift, six days a week. Single mom, two kids in elementary school, ex-husband who pays late. Hair in a tight high ponytail, kept off the grill. Has worked for Marge for twelve years and could open her own diner if she could get the loan. The oldest friend Maya makes in Millhaven, made fast because Cookie has no time for the slow version.

## Voice — sentence-length signature

**Mid-length, fast, profane.** Talks while she works — over the grill, between tickets, on the back step during the smoke break. Drops Maya's name a lot but uses it warmly. *"Maya, listen."* Not Frank's *"Maya."* — completely different mouth.

DO write:
> "Maya, listen, the trucker who comes in Thursday — wedding ring, doesn't matter, tips on the bill not on the table because his wife checks the receipts. Stand by his shoulder and refill the coffee twice and you'll get six instead of two."

DO NOT write:
> "Maya, I'd like to share some advice about a particular customer if you don't mind." — too formal; Cookie launches.

## Voice across phases

| Phase | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Pre-vouch (first 1-2 shifts)** | Friendly but watching. Tests her on the line. *"You can carry four plates. Try four-and-a-coffee."* |
| **Post-vouch (B11 cookie_peer_established)** | Names the regulars to her on the back step. Vouches for her to Marge. Maya is *in*. |
| **Working relationship (Ch3+)** | Bawdy, fast, generous. Comments on customers without restraint. Maya is the audience for the running monologue Cookie maintains as a survival mechanism. |
| **Late-Phase 1 — Maya escalates to T2/T3** | Notices. Doesn't moralize. *"Get yours."* The cook-version of Marge's silent management. |

## Body tells

- **Hair pulled tight** — never loose, never on the line
- **Spatula in left hand, tongs in right**
- **Wipes hands on the apron front, never on a towel** — towels are for plates
- **Sits on the back step for the smoke break**, never out front
- **Phone in apron pocket**, checked at the bell — kid texts
- **The dishwasher noise covers most conversation**, so she leans close on the line

## Vocabulary range

Working-class, profane, fast. *"Six-top by the window's a write-off, the husband's drunk." "Eighty the eight-top, push the meatloaf, it's been on the line forty minutes." "Goddamn this fryer."* Trade vocabulary like Marge but more of it because she's the cook.

## Signature phrases

- *"Maya, listen."* — opens any time she has intel to share
- *"Get yours."* — Cookie's acknowledgment that Maya knows what she's doing, no judgment
- *"This goddamn..."* — common opener for any equipment failure
- *"Marge'll tell you, but I'll tell you faster."* — Cookie's positioning

## Internal stance — what Cookie knows

Cookie has been in the diner twelve years. She knows every regular by tip habit, marriage status, and the shape of how they look at her girls. She talks because she has no other adult to talk to — her ex isn't worth the breath, her kids are kids, and Marge is Marge (i.e., does not gossip). Maya inherits Cookie's whole mental Rolodex by week three. This is the asset.

Cookie's voice should always *transfer information* — about a customer, about the line, about Marge, about how to make the money work. She's an informant in the friendliest sense.

## Specimen lines

Pre-vouch (testing):
> "You good with four plates? Try four and a coffee. Don't drop the coffee, it's been a week."

Post-vouch (back step, naming a regular):
> "Trucker comes in Thursday. Pete. Wife checks the receipts so he tips on the bill not on the table — but the bill's gonna land at twelve and he'll round it to twenty if you stand by his shoulder twice. Don't stand by his shoulder if you don't want to. Marge'd give you the eight-top instead. Just so you know."

Working relationship (running monologue between tickets):
> "Eight-top by the window, the husband's already drunk. Wife's gonna wanna split the check. They're gonna leave a fifteen-percent on a hundred-and-twenty and pretend it was twenty. I'm not putting the dessert on the bill. They didn't earn the dessert."

Post-T2 escalation (no judgment):
> "Get yours."

## Cookie-line failure modes — DON'T write these

- ❌ "I'm worried about you, hon" — Cookie isn't worried
- ❌ "You should be careful with these guys" — Cookie doesn't moralize, she informs
- ❌ "Marge wouldn't like that" — Cookie isn't a snitch
- ❌ Long lyrical sentences about Maya's interior — Cookie doesn't have time
- ❌ Any line where Cookie talks about her own feelings without it being a one-liner
