Make them care before the turn

Stakes, and two tools that hand them to the audience in the first thirty seconds.

Today’s win: you will add stakes to one of your own stories in two sentences.

Warm-up from Lesson 1

The idea

Your five-second moment only hits if the audience already cares how things turn out. That caring is stakes. The Moth’s test is one question: what do you stand to gain or lose? If you can’t say why the events mattered to you, their advice is to pick a different story.

The Moth’s Storytelling Tips, summarized with The Moth’s guidebook in getAbstract and NPR Life Kit.

Stakes aren’t the size of the event.
They are what the event means to you.

The Moth’s own example: losing your luggage at the airport is an ordinary annoyance. It becomes a story when the bag held the only photograph of your grandmother. Same event; now something can be lost. Stakes can also be inside you (guilt, pride, fear of being found out), not only outside.

Luggage example and internal/external stakes: The Moth’s tips, as quoted in journalism.co.uk and KNPR: What makes a Moth story a Moth story?

Two tools

Matthew Dicks names several ways to raise stakes. Start with these two; they work in the first thirty seconds.

The Elephant

Say early, plainly, what the problem is or what you need. The audience now knows what to worry about. Dicks puts it as early in the story as possible.

The Backpack

Tell the audience your plan, hope or fear before the moment happens. They carry it with you, so when things go differently, they feel it too.

Dicks, Storyworthy (2018); described in Manas Saloi’s book notes and SuperSummary. Dicks’s others (Breadcrumbs, Hourglass, Crystal Ball) come in later lessons.

See it work: your backend story

The details below are invented to show the shape. Swap in your real ones.

Flat

  1. Six months into my first job, our backend lead left.
  2. He made me the new lead.
  3. I realised I was a good developer.

With an Elephant and a Backpack

  1. Every pull request I opened, I tagged three people to review it. Just in case.
  2. Six months into my first job, our backend lead told us he was leaving, and nobody knew who would own the servers. (Elephant)
  3. My plan was simple: keep my head down, let them hire someone senior, don’t break anything. (Backpack)
  4. On his last Friday he called me in and said, “The backend’s yours.”

Same moment in line 4. In the second version you hear it as someone who was hoping to hide; that is why it lands.

Your drill

Write it, send it in chat

Pick your paneer roll story. It has the smallest stakes of your three, which makes it the best practice. Small stakes are fine; they just have to be yours.

  1. Elephant: one sentence that says what was at stake for you about money back then. (Guilt? A family reason? A goal you were saving for?)
  2. Backpack: one sentence with your plan or rule that night, before you ordered.
