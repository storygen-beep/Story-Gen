Storytelling · Reference · Lesson 1

Story anatomy

One page. Check before you tell.

PieceWhat it isTest
AnecdoteA single moment: a thing happened.Nobody changed? Anecdote.
StoryA moment with stakes and a change: before ≠ after.Can you say who you were before and after?
Five-second momentThe instant your understanding shifts. One per story. Small is great.Finish: “I realized…”
OpeningThe opposite of the five-second moment.Does line 1 show the “before” you?
StakesWhat could be won or lost.“A story without stakes is an essay.”

Build order

  1. Find the five-second moment.
  2. Write its opposite: that is your first line.
  3. Keep only what moves from the first line to the moment.

Raising stakes

ToolWhat it isWhere
The Moth testWhat do you stand to gain or lose? No answer, pick another story.Before you tell
ElephantName the problem or need, plainly.As early as possible
BackpackYour plan, hope or fear, before it meets reality.Just before the turn

Stakes are what the event means to you, not how big it is. They can be inside you: guilt, pride, fear.

Endings

RuleWhy
Write the last line first; memorize only the first and last lines.You never wander looking for the exit.
Put the five-second moment near the end.Little should follow the turn.
End on the after-state: one concrete “ever since then.”Shows the change; the audience draws the lesson.
No broad concept, no “and that’s my story.”A moral anyone could say belongs to no one.

Daily habit

Homework for Life: each night, write the day’s most storyworthy moment in a sentence or two.
