Lesson 0001 · Vegetable export from Indore

From mandi to port: the map

Three papers make you a legal vegetable exporter in India. Four roles decide how much money you put at risk. Learn both, then pick where to stand.

Your win: name the three papers and who issues each, and say which role you could start in with the money you have.

Why Indore is a good place to stand

Madhya Pradesh grew 17.17% of India's onions in 2023-24, second only to Maharashtra at 35.45%.[APEDA] It is also India's top garlic state, with over two million tonnes in FY2022.[Statista/APEDA] Around Indore, garlic is a Rabi crop, sown mid-October to mid-November.[Down to Earth]

So the produce is already near you. Onions, garlic and potatoes all sit under HS chapter 07 (vegetables), and onions and garlic share heading 0703. You will see those codes on every shipping bill.

The three papers

Once

IEC · Importer Exporter Code

Issued by DGFT, online, using your PAN and bank details. No export or import happens without it. Most guides put the fee at ₹500; confirm on the DGFT site before paying.[APEDA Ready Reckoner]

Every 5 yrs

RCMC from APEDA

Fresh fruits and vegetables are "scheduled products", so APEDA registration is mandatory to export them. You apply online with your IEC and get a Registration-cum-Membership Certificate.[APEDA procedure]

Every shipment

Phytosanitary certificate

Proof from India's plant quarantine service that this consignment is pest-free to the buyer country's rules. Applied for on the PQMS portal. Perishables are usually certified in 24-48 hours; fumigated lots take up to 3 days.[Lok Sabha]

Policy moves fast. Onion exports were restricted in late 2023, reopened in May 2024 with a USD 550/tonne minimum export price, and that minimum was removed in September 2024.[Lokmat/ANI] Before any deal, check DGFT notifications that the product is "free" to export today.

The four roles

The papers above belong to whoever is the exporter on the shipping bill. Not every role in the trade is that person.

RoleWhat you doYour money at risk
Grower-exporterGrow the crop, pack and ship it yourself.Highest: land, crop, stock, buyer payment.
Merchant exporterBuy from mandis or farmers, grade, pack, ship.High: you own the stock until the buyer pays.
Sourcing agentFind and inspect produce for a foreign buyer or an exporter; earn a commission.Low: you never own the goods.
Service providerCustoms broking, freight forwarding, packhouse, cold chain.Varies; customs broking needs a Customs licence.

Only the first two need all three papers. That is the key fact for choosing: the further down the table, the less capital you need and the less you earn per tonne.

Check yourself

Answer from memory. Don't scroll up first; the effort is what makes it stick.

1. Which paper do you get once, before any export at all?

The IEC from DGFT is the one-time key. The phytosanitary certificate is per shipment; a bill of lading is a shipping document, not a registration.

2. Fresh vegetables are an APEDA "scheduled product". What follows?

Scheduled means APEDA registration (the RCMC) is mandatory for the exporter of record.

3. How often do you need a phytosanitary certificate?

It certifies one consignment's plant health, so each shipment gets its own, through PQMS. Five years is the RCMC renewal.

4. Onion exports swung from restricted to free within a year. What do you do before a deal?

Export policy for onions is changed by DGFT notification, sometimes with immediate effect. Check it every time.

5. Little capital, but you know Indore mandi traders well. Cheapest role to start?

A sourcing agent never owns the goods, so almost nothing is at risk, and mandi contacts are the asset that role sells.
