Lesson 0002 · Vegetable export from Indore

How a sourcing agent gets paid

You never own the onions. What you sell is knowing which mandi lot is good and which exporter ships on time. The skill is getting paid for that, every time.

Your win: sketch one deal: who pays you, on what amount, when, and the two things that can stop you getting paid.

Quick recall from lesson 1

Before reading on, say out loud: which three papers does the exporter need, and why does a sourcing agent need none of them? (Answer at the bottom of lesson 1's role table.)

Two sides you can work for

Buyer's agent

You work for the foreign importer

A buyer in Dubai or Dhaka wants 25 tonnes of Indore onions. You find an Indian exporter, check the lot, watch the loading. The buyer pays your commission, in foreign currency.

Seller's agent

You work for the Indian exporter

An exporter with IEC and RCMC needs buyers or a steady supply of graded produce. You bring them, and the exporter pays you in rupees.

Either way, the exporter of record ships the goods and holds the papers from lesson 1. You hold an agreement.

The commission: rate, base, timing

  • Rate. I found no official benchmark for Indian fresh-produce agents. Figures quoted elsewhere run from about 1% of invoice value (a forum remark) to 5-15% (a general B2B agent figure, not vegetables).[guide][Alibaba] Treat both as unconfirmed and ask real agents.
  • Base. The percentage is taken either on the invoice value or on the net proceeds actually realised.[guide] Net proceeds is smaller if the buyer claims a discount for spoiled produce.
  • Timing. Exporters often pay the agent only after the buyer's money reaches India.[guide] With vegetables, that means a quality dispute at the far end can delay or cut your pay.

One container earns little. An agent's income comes from repeat orders, so reliable quality matters more than any single price.

Risk one: being cut out

Once you introduce a buyer to an exporter, they have each other's phone numbers. Nothing in the export papers mentions you. Your only protection is a written agreement that names the parties you introduced and promises you commission on their future deals. Have an Indian trade lawyer draft it; I found no official template to point you to.

Risk two: GST on your commission

Indian GST calls an agent who arranges a sale between two other parties an "intermediary". Under Section 13(8)(b) of the IGST Act, the place of supply for an intermediary is where the agent is, so commission from a foreign buyer did not count as an export of services and could attract GST.[Tally]

This may have just changed. One commentary reports that the Finance Act, 2026 removed Section 13(8)(b), so the place of supply becomes the buyer's location.[Grant Thornton] I could only find that in one source. Ask a chartered accountant in Indore before you invoice your first commission.

Where agents meet buyers

  • APEDA buyer-seller meets. APEDA and VAFA (Fresh Vegetables & Fruits Exporters Association) are running a reverse buyer-seller meet with foreign buyers at the Fresh India Show 2026 in Mumbai.[FreshPlaza]
  • APEDA's service providers directory lists packhouses, labs and others, the people you will coordinate.
  • Indore exporters who already hold IEC and RCMC. They are your supply side; the MPIDC export cell in Indore runs meetups where you can find them.

Check yourself

From memory, no scrolling.

1. As a sourcing agent, who ships the goods and holds the IEC?

The agent never owns or ships the goods. The exporter of record files the shipping bill and holds IEC, RCMC and the phytosanitary certificate.

2. Paid on net proceeds, the buyer claims a quality discount. What happens to you?

Net proceeds means what actually arrives. A discount for spoiled produce cuts the base, so it cuts your share too.

3. What protects you after the buyer and exporter have met?

Export papers never mention the agent. Only a written agreement records who you introduced and what you are owed.

4. Why might commission from a Dubai buyer attract Indian GST?

Section 13(8)(b) put an intermediary's place of supply at the agent's location, so it was not an export of services. The Finance Act, 2026 may have changed this; confirm with a CA.
