Lesson 0003 · Vegetable export from Indore

Checking a lot at the mandi

A buyer abroad cannot see the onions. You can. Your check before loading is the service you sell, and your written report is what protects your commission later.

Your win: check a sample of onions on five points, work out the percentages, and decide pass or fail against the buyer's spec.

Quick recall from lesson 2

Say it before reading on: if you are paid on net proceeds and the buyer claims a quality discount, what happens to your commission? (Lesson 2, "The commission".)

That is why this lesson exists. A dated report with photos, made before loading, is your answer to a quality claim that arrives three weeks later.

Whose rules?

  • Agmark (India). Onion grades are set in Schedule XIX of the Fruits and Vegetables Grading and Marking Rules, 2004. For export, a Certificate of Agmark Grading is issued per lot.[WDRA/Agmark]
  • UNECE FFV-25 (Europe and others). The international onion standard many importers follow.[UNECE 2017]
  • The buyer's spec. In practice the buyer names a size band and limits in the contract. Trade listings quote bands such as 40-60 mm or 46-65 mm, and they vary.[listing] Get the spec in writing before you check anything.

The limits below come from those standards. Your buyer's written spec replaces them wherever it says something different.

The five checks

1 · Size

Widest diameter across the middle

Onion size is the maximum diameter of the equatorial section: measure across the widest part, never top to root.[EU 1508/2001] A sizing tolerance of 10% of the sample is allowed under both Agmark and UNECE.[Agmark][FFV-25]

2 · Spread

Smallest vs largest in one bag

Agmark caps the gap between the smallest and largest onion in the same package.[Agmark] A bag of mixed sizes signals a lot that was not graded.

3 · Sound

Firm, intact, no rot

Squeeze and cut a few. Under UNECE Class II, at most 2% may be decayed, even inside the general 10% tolerance.[UNECE]

4 · Sprout

Green shoots showing

Sprouting worsens in transit. A draft Codex standard allows 4% by weight with visible shoots.[Codex draft] It is a draft, so use the buyer's figure if they give one.

5 · Clean

Root tufts and soil

UNECE minimum requirements ask for onions that are reasonably firm and nearly free of root tufts.[UNECE]

How to sample

This is a working habit, not an official sampling plan. Pull bags from the top, middle and bottom of the stack, never only the ones the trader hands you. Count out 100 onions, so every count is already a percentage. Photograph each defect next to a ruler, and photograph the bag numbers.

For EU shipments there is a formal route: lots are sampled at an APEDA/Agmark-recognised lab or pack house, and the container is loaded under that lab's supervision.[APEDA]

Practice: run the numbers

A lot from an Indore trader. You sampled 100 onions. The example counts are filled in; change them and watch the verdict. Limits default to the standards above; type the buyer's figures over them.

Defaults: size 10% (Agmark, UNECE), decay 2% (UNECE Class II), sprouting 4% (draft Codex). Counts are simplified to number of onions; some standards count by weight.

In the example, rot fails at 3% against 2%. Your job is to say so before loading, with photos, not after the buyer complains.

Check yourself

From memory. Question 5 comes from lesson 2 on purpose.

1. How do you measure an onion's size?

Size is the maximum diameter of the equatorial section, the widest point across the middle.

2. 100 sampled, 12 outside the buyer's band, 10% tolerance. Verdict?

12% is over the 10% tolerance. Off-size onions are allowed, just not that many.

3. Class II lot: 100 sampled, 3 rotting. Verdict?

Decay has its own 2% limit inside the 10% general tolerance. 3% fails.

4. Agmark, UNECE and your buyer disagree on size. What sets the band?

The written spec is what the buyer will judge the goods against when they arrive, so it sets the band.

5. Lesson 2 recall: who ships the goods and holds the IEC?

The exporter of record. You check and report; they ship and hold the papers.
