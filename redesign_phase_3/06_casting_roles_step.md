# Step 3 — Casting (roles + hooks): every NPC earns their place

The step between the story/engine (Step 2) and the per-NPC arcs (Step 4). It's the answer to LO's
"just randomly adding NPCs isn't good." **Before anyone designs an NPC's arc, that NPC must have a ROLE
in the story/cascade and a HOOK that makes them desirable.** No role → cut or merge. This is both a
*generative* step (derive the roles the game needs, cast people into them) and a *filter* (reject any
roleless or off-fantasy NPC). It answers directly to the good-game qualities (`05`): coherence (#8),
desirable characters (#5), the fantasy (#1).

**Pipeline position (renumber):** 0 good-game · 1 setup · 2 top-level(story+engine) · **3 CASTING** ·
4 NPC arcs · 5 content roster · 6 authoring.

---

## The principle
Like a novelist casting a story: characters aren't added at random — each serves the plot/theme. Here
the **core fantasy + the cascade GENERATE the role slots the game needs**; you then cast the setup's
named people into those slots, give each a hook, and cut anyone who doesn't fit. Every NPC answers
**"what am I FOR in this game?"** before getting an arc.

---

## Inputs
- The **core fantasy + desire curve** (`05`) — the thing every NPC must serve.
- The **progression engine / cascade** (`04`) — it *demands* certain structural roles.
- The **cast named at setup** (just people so far — `01`) + the **world/locations**.

Casting may **amend the cast**: ADD an NPC to fill a missing required role; CUT or MERGE a roleless one.

---

## The role taxonomy (derive which you need — don't hardcode the set)
Three tiers. The cascade makes the first tier near-mandatory; the fantasy drives the rest. Per the
no-hardcode philosophy (`04 §7`): include a role only when the game's fantasy/engine actually needs it.

**A. Structural roles — the cascade won't run without these:**
- **Pressure source** — creates the money/survival squeeze that drives Act 1 (the loan shark, the landlord, the debt). Without it, nothing pushes the player to corrupt themselves. *(Near-mandatory.)*
- **Corrupting on-ramp** — supplies the self-corruption opportunities (the boss who offers the risqué job, the supplier). Fuels the self-corruption loop. *(Near-mandatory; can be the same character as a target or pressure source.)*

**B. Desire roles — the point of the game (the fantasy lives here):**
- **Core target(s)** — the 1–3 deep NPCs who ARE the fantasy's centerpiece; full two-meter arcs (the gold NPCs, P5). *At least one is mandatory* — it's what the player most wants. The fantasy's heart.
- **Peripheral target(s)** — lighter NPCs adding breadth/variety (a flirt, a fling); light model (P5).
- **Gatekeeper** — a target locked behind a *secondary* stat (charisma/status/fitness), which gives that stat a real job AND an aspirational prize. *(Include only if a secondary stat needs the job.)*

**C. Optional drama roles — add only if the fantasy calls for them:**
- **Ally / enabler** (gives info/access), **Rival** (competes for a target or status), **Witness/antagonist** (the scandal/awareness arc).

*One character can hold more than one role* (the boss = corrupting on-ramp AND a core target). Count
falls out of the fantasy + cascade, never a fixed number.

---

## The HOOK (what makes a character desirable — quality #5)
Every cast NPC gets a one-line **hook** = **a specific charged dynamic + a WANT**. The hook must carry:
- **the dynamic** (who they are to the player — boss / step-sis / jaded regular / landlord),
- **the charge** (what's forbidden/hot about them — the taboo this character delivers),
- **their want** (what *they* are after — so they have agency, can resist/scheme, aren't a yes-man),
- **their fantasy lane** (so the cast spans VARIETY — nurturing vs forbidden vs dominant vs reluctant).

A hook is "the strict bar-owner's wife you want to break, who's bored and craves danger" — NOT
"Family arc-shape #3." If you can't write the hook in one hungry line, the character isn't ready.

**The hook names its desire FLAVOR** (`15` Findings A/B): longing / transactional heat / **conquest** (you want
to *take/break/own* them). A conquest-target (e.g. the man you steal the bar from) is a legitimate core target as
long as the pursuit is hot and they have agency — not a cold tool. The cast as a whole must deliver the **desire
span declared at Step 0a** (`05` 0a-3): the right genders + flavors across the acts, on purpose.

---

## What casting assigns per NPC (the bridge to Step 4)
For each cast member, record: **role(s) · hook · fantasy lane · depth (core vs peripheral) · arc-shape**
(which implies the personal trait that will drive them — the spine menu, `02_GAME_SURVEY`) **· a rough
sketch** (below). Step 4 (NPC arcs) then details each one's locks/scenes; casting just sets *who they
are and what they're for.*

## The rough sketch (where each NPC roughly goes — and where cross-NPC lives) (`20` decision 7)
Alongside the role + hook, give each NPC a **rough sketch** — a loose few lines of where the character
*roughly* heads (not the full arc; that's Step 4). The sketch is the home for the game's **cross-NPC
connections**, kept deliberately light: *"she's got a friend who could become her own arc," "if she
ends up pregnant it changes how Sal treats you," "she and Dee already know each other."* These are
**ideas, not a format** — RTS-style organic threads (the brother's pregnancy rippling to the others; a
sister's friend opening into an arc), **optional, not every NPC** (islands are fine).

Why the sketch earns its place:
- **You sketch the whole cast in one sitting** → you *see the connections* while everyone's in front of
  you (the only "look at the forest" moment the one-at-a-time Step 4 can't give). No separate weave
  step needed.
- **Step 4 is never authored in a vacuum** — the sketch + its threads are already sitting there when you
  go deep on one NPC.
- **Three-level zoom, each seeding the next:** hook (one line) → **rough sketch (casting)** → full arc
  brief (Step 4, the sketch grows into §1 end-state).
- **When a thread grows up:** a friend-who-becomes-playable is a **new NPC** back through casting; a
  ripple is a **`cross_npc` beat** at authoring (`beat-authoring.md`). No system, no diagram — the
  sketch holds the idea; the existing machinery realizes it if it earns its place.

---

## The casting self-check (run before locking the cast)
- **Structural coverage:** is there a pressure source? a corrupting on-ramp? at least ONE core target? (The cascade is dead without all three.)
- **Every NPC has a role AND a hook** — no roleless character (cut/merge), no shapeless one (no hook → not ready).
- **Coherence (#8):** every NPC serves the core fantasy — no off-theme character.
- **Variety:** the cast spans different fantasy lanes/dynamics — not five of the same. (Each core target a *distinct* desire.)
- **Desire span delivered** (`15` B): the cast actually covers the genders + desire flavors declared at Step 0a
  (`05` 0a-3) — and any conquest-target reads as wanted-as-conquest, not as a cold instrument (`05` #5).
- **Pressure escalates across acts** (`15` E): there's an Act-1 survival pressure source AND a late-act
  empire-threat (rival / cop / boss) so the squeeze never dies — not just one debt that vanishes when she's rich.
- **No redundancy:** two NPCs with the same role + same hook → merge or differentiate.
- **Desire spread:** there's a clear "favorite-able" core target that *is* the fantasy.
- **The 3-question LC test (`05`):** can you say the fantasy in one sentence? name what the player wants next? name *each* NPC's hook? If any NPC fails the third, fix or cut it.

## Output artifact (a `## Casting` section in `design_book.md`)
| NPC | role(s) | hook (dynamic + charge + want) | lane | depth | arc-shape | rough sketch (+ cross-NPC threads) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
One row per NPC. The **rough sketch** is a loose few lines (not a table cell's worth — write it out
under the table if it needs room), carrying any cross-NPC threads. This is the input to Step 4 (NPC
arcs) and the coherence record for the whole game.

## Worked example (the bar game)
Core fantasy: *"Turn the dive bar you inherited into your own den of corruption — own everyone in it."*
The cascade + fantasy generate the slots; we cast:
- **Boyd** — *pressure source*: the loan shark who'll seize the bar; hook = "the smiling shark squeezing you, who'd take the bar — or other payment." Drives Act 1.
- **Dee** — *corrupting on-ramp* + peripheral: the supplier who deals only when you're desperate; hook = "the coercive supplier who likes you cornered."
- **Sal** — *core target*: hook = "your late partner's steady best friend, loyal and guilt-ridden, who's wanted you for years and hates himself for it." The emotional heart. (slow-burn arc-shape.)
- **Marcus** — *peripheral target*: hook = "the smooth regular who treats the bar like his hunting ground — easy, transactional heat." (dating/transactional arc-shape.)
- **The standoffish new regular** — *gatekeeper* (charisma-locked): hook = "the cold out-of-towner who won't give a broke nobody the time of day — until you're someone."
Every NPC is load-bearing. A proposed sixth bartender with no role and no hook → **cut** (or given a job).
