# What makes a GOOD game in this genre (the head of the pipeline)

The piece every prior step was missing. A structurally-correct game (LC/LS — lanes, gates, schedules,
builds green) can still be *not good* — "a correct skeleton with no soul" (LO). This doc defines GOOD
as concrete, designable qualities, grounded in evidence, so it can LEAD the pipeline: the core fantasy +
the desire curve come first, and setup / cascade / casting / arcs / roster all become answerable to them.

## Evidence base (grounded, not taste)
- **Authoritative game-design wisdom** (gamedeveloper.com, Ernest Adams via Wikipedia sandbox criticism, pacing/grind articles) — *high confidence, directly transferable.*
- **Adult-specific player signal** (RTS itch.io comments/rating 3.9; "Become Someone" reviews; recommendation/community snippets) — *thinner (adult forums aren't search-indexed) but consistent.*
- **The 10-game survey** (`02_GAME_SURVEY_top_level.md`) — what the good ones actually do.
- **The LC/LS failure analysis** — the negative example.
Sources: gamedeveloper.com (grind/pacing), en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbox_game (Adams/Totten critique), astk.itch.io/roadtosuccess (RTS player comments), twinfinite/steamah (Become Someone).

## The core: the genre runs on DESIRE
A porn sandbox is good when it sustains **desire** — it makes you *want* something, keeps the next
wanted thing *visible*, and makes *getting* it feel earned and hot. "Good" = **sustained, legible,
rewarded desire toward a juicy fantasy.** Every quality below serves that; every "enemy" kills it.

---

## Step 0a — POV gate + the good-fantasy bar (do this FIRST, above everything)
The fantasy is the head of the whole pipeline: a generic fantasy guarantees a forgettable game no matter
how good the machinery. So before any quality below, two gates — *learned from the dry-run* (`08`), where
applying only "say it in a sentence" still produced a generic premise ("inherit a house of women").

**(1) Pick the PROTAGONIST POV first — it decides which fantasies even work.**
Fantasy quality is POV-dependent. The same hook can be great for one POV and wrong-shaped for the other:
- **Male PC** → acquisition / power / harem fantasies ("inherit a house of women and make them yours" is
  a *fine male-PC* fantasy).
- **Female PC** → self-corruption / rise-via-seduction / being-desired-and-transformed / becoming-the-one-in-power
  (madam, queen). **RTS is a female PC**, and our cascade ("corrupt yourself before others") is
  **female-PC-native** — it's literally RTS's model.
Choose the POV, *then* choose a fantasy that resonates for it. A male-PC harem fantasy on a female PC
(or vice-versa) is wrong-shaped, not just weak.

**(2) The fantasy must clear the THREE-PART bar** (not just "is it a fantasy?"):
- **POV-fit** — chosen *for* this protagonist's POV.
- **A sharp specific charge** — a *reversal* (powerless → queen), a *taboo*, or a *transformation* —
  NOT generic acquisition ("have all the women"). The charge is the engine of desire.
- **Carries its own two-act shape** — the fantasy *implies its own progression*; you don't staple a
  cascade on afterward. (A strong fantasy tells you the acts.)

**(3) Declare the DESIRE SPAN** (added from dry-run #2, `15` Finding B).
Before casting, state *which registers the fantasy covers* across its acts — the **genders** of the core
targets and the **flavor of desire** (romantic longing / transactional heat / **conquest** — wanting to
break-and-own a person). A fantasy can legitimately span more than one (the bar→empire fantasy is M in Act 1
→ F in Act 2 → conquest-flavored throughout). The point is it must be **chosen, not stumbled into**: a player
who came for one register shouldn't be ambushed by another. Write it down so the cast is built to deliver it.

**The test:** *"would someone pick this over the ten other inherit-the-house games?"* If not, it's not ready.

**Worked contrast (from the dry-run):**
- ✗ "Inherit your father's estate and make the women yours." Passes "one sentence," but generic
  acquisition, no sharp charge, no built-in shape → a competent-but-forgettable game.
- ✓ **(female PC)** "Broke waitress at a sleazy bar a man owns → seduce and corrupt *him* until you take
  the bar from under him → then build it into your own empire, recruiting and corrupting other women into
  your stable until you're the madam the city answers to." POV-fit ✓; sharp charge (power reversal +
  seduction-as-weapon) ✓; built-in two acts (take the man → build the empire) ✓.

This is the canonical example for the rest of Phase 3.

## The 8 qualities of a good game (each: what it is · evidence · the check · the LC/LS failure it prevents)

1. **A sharp core fantasy** — one specific, juicy thing the whole game is *about*, that clears the
   **Step-0a bar** above (POV-fit + sharp charge + built-in two-act shape) — not just "a fantasy, not a setting."
   - *Evidence:* survey — good games sell one hungry hook; "treat it maturely → explore characters with depth." Dry-run (`08`): the one-sentence test alone lets generic premises through.
   - *Check:* run the Step-0a three-part bar + the test "would someone pick this over ten other inherit-the-house games?"
   - *Prevents:* LC = "a bar with NPCs" (a setting); AND the subtler trap — a *technically-valid but generic* fantasy (the estate) that yields a well-built forgettable game.

2. **A legible pull (you always know what you want next, and how to get it)** — the next unlock is *visible* and the path to it is *clear*.
   - *Evidence:* RTS's #1 player complaint = unclear progression / misleading guide / trial-and-error; Adams: "plunking the player in a sandbox and saying 'have fun' isn't enough" — the killer is no satisfying goals.
   - *Check:* at any moment, can the player name what they're working toward and the next step? Is locked content *shown* (telegraphed), not hidden?
   - *Prevents:* LC/LS = systems with no goal-pull; you don't know what to chase.

3. **No grind (multiple ways to make progress)** — never a single wall you must repeat a task against.
   - *Evidence:* grind = padding (gamedeveloper.com); fix = "design multiple forms of progression — if blocked on X, progress on Y"; even RTS players beg for skip/cheats to cut grind.
   - *Check:* if one track is blocked, is there always another that moves? Is any unlock pure repeat-the-same-action padding?
   - *Prevents:* the universal enemy; *validates our distributed multi-trait gating* (`04 §5`) as a GOODNESS principle, not just structure.

4. **Constant reward drip + payoff** — every beat grants something that makes you want to keep going; big moments LAND.
   - *Evidence:* "ensure beating a level always grants a reward, pushing the player to see what's next"; dopamine loop = "small accomplishments compel you to keep playing"; pacing = alternate buildup/payoff.
   - *Check:* does every action return *something* (progress shown, a tease, a tier)? Do the capstones pay off generously (Tier-3 prose, the moment lands)?
   - *Prevents:* LC = meters climb, nothing pays off → no reason to care.

5. **Characters you desire (a hook, not a shape)** — each NPC is a specific charged dynamic with a *want*; you have a favorite and chase her/him.
   - *Desire has more than one flavor* (`15` Finding A): romantic longing, transactional heat, OR **conquest** —
     wanting to *break, own, bring to heel* a person. In a power-rise fantasy the core target is often pursued as a
     **conquest** (you want to take the man, not bed-him-for-love) — and that is *as charged as* lust, **as long as
     the pursuit is hot and the target has real agency** (resists, schemes, cracks). Don't force a heist into a love
     story; play the conquest *as* the desire.
   - *Evidence:* "Become Someone's RPG elements + girls who react to you got me hooked"; mature treatment → character depth.
   - *Check:* does each NPC have a one-line *hook* (the boss you want to break, the oblivious step-sis) — not "Family
     arc-shape #3"? Distinct voice? Is the *flavor* of desire (longing / heat / conquest) named, and the pursuit hot in that flavor?
   - *Prevents:* LC's interchangeable arc-shapes → nobody pulls you; AND the cold-instrumental trap — a target you
     *use* but never *want* (play the conquest as desire, not as errand).

6. **Earned escalation, paced (tension → release)** — work a little, pay off; alternate charge and breather; always feel progress.
   - *Evidence:* pacing principles — alternate high-energy/quiet, always feel you're progressing; "just difficult enough."
   - *Check:* is the heat a *curve* (buildup → payoff → new buildup), not flat and not all-at-once? Is the first kiss earned, the big scene a peak?
   - *Prevents:* LC's flat grind OR everything-available-at-once (no tension).

7. **The charge (lean into the taboo)** — confident transgression; the specific kink/power is the point, delivered at full intensity.
   - *Evidence:* genre runs on transgression; our own max-explicit doctrine (`doctrine/08`); tepid = forgettable.
   - *Check:* does the game commit to its charge at the moments that matter (no soft-pedaling at the peak)?
   - *Prevents:* the safe, lukewarm scene that kills the fantasy.

8. **A reactive, coherent world** — your growth is acknowledged (people treat you differently, the world reflects your state) and everything serves the one fantasy.
   - *Evidence:* "girls react to you differently depending on your stats" (Become Someone) = the hook; sandbox coherence critique (Totten) — random content gets old.
   - *Check:* does the world visibly change as you progress? Does every NPC/activity serve the core fantasy (coherence — no random NPCs, `06_…` casting)?
   - *Prevents:* LC's static wallpaper + assembled-not-composed feel.

## The enemies (what makes a game BAD — the failure checklist)
- **No goal / no pull** — a sandbox that says "have fun." (#1 killer.)
- **Grind / padding** — single-track repetition to unlock.
- **Illegibility** — player doesn't know what to do next or what they're working toward.
- **No payoff** — meters climb, nothing lands.
- **Interchangeable characters** — shapes, not hooks.
- **Flat or dumped pacing** — no tension curve.
- **Tepidness** — soft-pedaling the charge.
- **Incoherence** — random NPCs/content, no unifying fantasy.
LC/LS hit most of these. RTS mostly avoids them (its remaining sins = grind + legibility, the two things even the north-star gets dinged for).

## How this HEADS the pipeline
This becomes **Step 0** — above setup. The first thing authored is **(0a) the POV + the core fantasy**
(through the three-part bar), then **the desire curve** (what the player wants, how the pull stays
legible, where the payoffs land). Then:
- **Setup** inherits the POV from 0a and picks a premise that *delivers the fantasy* (not just a setting).
- **The cascade** (`04`) is the *mechanism* of the desire curve (fall → reach → deepen = buildup → payoff).
- **Casting** (`06`) ensures every NPC is a *hook* serving the fantasy (coherence).
- **Content roster** fills the *reward drip* (always something next).
- **Authoring** spends the *payoff* prose where it lands.
Every downstream step carries a check from the 8 qualities; anything that fails one is cut or fixed.
**The test that would have caught LC:** "pick the POV / clear the 3-part fantasy bar / name what the
player wants next / name each NPC's hook" — LC fails all of them (and the estate dry-run fails the
fantasy bar despite passing the one-sentence test).
