# Pitching content — how to propose the next thing so it lands hot

Every junction in this pipeline ends with a proposal: which NPC next, what the next beat is, what the
expansion after the frontier looks like. `run-mode.md` ("Navigation at junctions") owns **when** you propose
and the Mode A/B discipline. This file owns **what makes the proposal good** — because a structurally
perfect pipeline that keeps proposing lukewarm content produces a competent, forgettable game.

**Scope.** This is the bar for every content pitch *after the seed*: a beat, an arc, an NPC, a chunk, a
whole act. The **game's** founding fantasy has its own gate — the 3-part bar at `step-0-1-seed.md` Step 0
(POV-fit · a sharp specific charge · carries its own two-act shape) — and this file does not restate it.
Step 0 fires once; this fires every time after.

**The law: pitch the DESIRE, not the plot that delivers it.** These are porn games. An idea whose pitch is
a sequence of events ("she infiltrates the archive, finds the ledger, confronts the buyer") is a plot idea
— it may be *structurally* excellent and still leave the player cold, because nothing in it says what the
player is going to *want*. At game scale the field's winning premises are all one sentence — **an ordinary
person + an instrument of power + an institution full of people to use it on** (a hypnosis app and your
hometown; a serum and the office; a magic taxi and a city of women) — and the same discipline scales down:
a *beat* pitch names the want, the lever, and the person it's used on ("she's the only one who takes his
hunt seriously, and that's the lever"). Either way you can see the erotic trajectory from the pitch alone.
That is what "hot" means at pitch level — not raunchier wording.

---

## The five parts

A pitch that's ready to build carries all five. Missing one is not a style problem; each missing part is a
specific way the built thing goes wrong.

1. **THE CHARGE** — one sentence naming the fantasy, with the **kink areas named out loud** (the areas in
   `kink-ceilings.md` §2 — and if the area's ceiling row is blank, §2 says the beat doesn't ship until it's
   declared). Not "a tense scene with Calloway" — *what is the player going to want here.*
2. **THE LADDER** — the rungs, and **the act it ends on**. State the destination: "…ending with him on his
   knees in his own study." A ladder with no stated destination is how escalation debt accumulates until
   players call an update insulting; naming the end also sizes the work honestly.
3. **THE PERSON** — who it runs on, and the **one trait that makes this corruption theirs** rather than a
   slot's. Players in this genre praise characters, never plots; the arcs the field remembers are the ones
   where a specific person's specific resistance is the thing being worn down.
4. **THE DEPOSIT** — what it feeds in the machine: which meter, quest, or growth state of the accumulation
   object (`step-2-toplevel.md` §4, §7). A beat that changes no state is a cutscene; the deposit is what
   makes it part of the game rather than an anecdote inside it.
5. **THE COST** — what it risks. Which fail-state form is live here (`content-framework.md` §1C: danger ·
   debt · deadline · decay), or the honest "nothing — this one is safe, and here's why that's right."
   Free escalation reads as a world that doesn't care.

### The heat test
**Can the reader see the erotic trajectory from the pitch alone?** If it only sounds hot once you imagine
the prose you'd write, it isn't a hot idea — it's a plot idea wearing lingerie. Rewrite it around the
desire, or drop it.

## Worked contrast (from `vesper` — third person, that game's locked register)

✗ **"The Archive — she gets inside Vance Securities, works Calloway for access to his file room, and
identifies the buyer."** Structurally fine: a place, an objective, a reveal. It's a heist pitch. Nothing in
it says what she wants, what he wants, or what the sex *is* — so the sex gets attached afterward as a
reward for finishing the plot, and the beat ships cold.

✓ **"She's the only person in that building who takes his hunt seriously — and a man who's been called a
crank for two years will hand over anything to the one person who believes him. Every rung is her feeding
the belief he's starving for, until the file room is hers. (Areas: seduction-as-weapon, an older man's
dignity spent on being wanted — ceiling per his row.) Ladder: she takes the hunt seriously → the late
nights working his files → the evening he stops pretending it's about the case → the night he brings her
the drawer himself and calls it help. Runs on Calloway: his one trait is that he's starving to be believed,
so every rung is belief, never charm — charm would bounce off him. Deposits: `npc_calloway.relation`
through Guarded → Believer → Attached, and the drains that open the buyer thread. Costs: his arc's last
stage is **Betrayed** — she's spending a man who will find out, and that bill is authored, not implied."**

Same plot. But the seduction IS the method, the destination is named, the person's *specific* hunger is
the engine (belief, not charm — which is why a generic seduction ladder would have failed on him), and it
hooks both the machine and the price.

The difference is not vocabulary. Both are RTS-flat. The second one can be *checked*, *sized*, and
*wanted* before a line is written.

## How to present it (LO's standing preference)

**Write the pitch as concrete, editable prose — not a menu of abstractions.** Give an honest recommendation
and the actual text he can cut into, so he's editing something real instead of ranking labels. Two or three
*fully written* alternatives beat six one-line options.

**This does not cancel Mode A.** Several pitch-shaped decisions are genuine identity forks that
`run-mode.md` says to ASK (who the core targets are and each one's hook, the economy shape, the
frontier/ending shape). Ask those — the rule here governs the *content* of what you put in front of him,
not whether you ask: an `AskUserQuestion` whose options are fully-written pitches is right; the same
question with four abstract labels is the thing to avoid.

**Pitch one thing at a time.** A junction proposal is the next piece, not a roadmap; the pipeline is
incremental on purpose (`run-mode.md`).

## Two anti-patterns the five parts don't already catch

(Each part above states its own failure mode — a missing ladder-destination, an unnamed ceiling, a slot
instead of a person, an orphan beat. These two are different: they look complete and still fail.)

- **The systems pitch.** "A reputation track that gates three tiers across the district" is a *mechanism*,
  not a pitch — it answers *how*, never what the player wants, and it will pass a structural review while
  producing nothing anyone wants to play. Mechanism belongs in the blueprint (Step 5), after the want.
- **The costless pitch.** All five parts present, cost written as "nothing at stake here" every time. One
  safe beat is fine; a whole game of them is the dead-world complaint (`content-framework.md` §1C). If the
  cost slot keeps coming up empty across a chunk, the pushback declaration is the thing that's wrong, not
  the pitch.

---

## Self-check (before you send a pitch)
- All five parts present: **charge · ladder · person · deposit · cost**.
- The kink areas are **named**, and each one's ceiling is declared (or the pitch says it needs declaring).
- The ladder states the **act it ends on**.
- It passes the **heat test** — the trajectory is visible from the pitch alone.
- It is **concrete prose he can edit**, not a menu of abstractions.
- The register stays RTS-flat even in the pitch — specific, not literary (`rts-flat-prose.md`).
