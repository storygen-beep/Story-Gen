# RTS-flat prose — the mechanical register rules

Read this when you write any scene body — the `paragraph` / `dialog` / `cascade` blocks inside a
canvas. It is the **HOW**: the numbered prose rules + the three density tiers + when each applies. The
**WHY** (density of decision-pressure over density of prose, the throttle/odometer model) lives in
`references/rts-design-philosophy.md` — don't re-derive it here. The **dialogue-MODE** axis (carry
character through `dialog` blocks, not narrated summary) is already stated well in `references/lanes.md`
"Voice register" — this file points to it, doesn't restate it.

**The contract is "specificity, not literary density."** RTS-flat means **terse + concrete +
re-readable** — NOT purple. Specific ("the runner Diana picked out") without being literary (no interior
monologue, no extended metaphor). There is **no word-count minimum and no sensory-grounding mandate** —
the opposite: when in doubt, default to MORE-flat / FEWER-words / FEWER-beats. This is adult content; at
sexual register be direct and crude with real anatomical terms — but the FLAT rules govern *density*,
not *whether the content is explicit*.

## §1 — Density follows the lane (the dual register)

Every canvas body sits in ONE of two voice registers. The lane (`references/lanes.md`) picks which:

| Lane | Register | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Hub buttons** | **RTS-flat default** | Player clicks these menu items repeatedly across the arc. Re-readable density. |
| **2 — Ambient encounters** | **RTS-flat default** | Each fires 10–20 times across an arc. Same scene, different stat tiers, same density. |
| **3 — Dispatcher walk-ins** | **RTS-flat default** | Dice-rolled inside a chore. Re-readable; specific detail allowed; literary density NOT. |
| **4 — Capstones** | **Tier-3 literary EARNED** | Once-only milestone. Player won't see it again — the single read justifies the spend. |

A Lane 2 ambient authored at Tier-3 costs the same density EACH time the player sees it, and by the third
read the language feels performative. A capstone fires ONCE — the prose can spend. That asymmetry is the
whole reason for the split.

**Rarity is its own punch — escalate a scarce beat by WEIGHT, not FREQUENCY.** When a beat lands *because* it's
rare (a once-only gut-punch, an involuntary crack, a glitch peak), resist giving it a recurring/ambient version —
recurrence dilutes the scarcity that was the whole effect. The rising-frequency saturation curve (`lanes.md`
Lane 2/3) is for **repeatable ambients**; it does NOT apply to a scripted rare beat. Make the *next* one hit
harder (weight/depth), don't make it happen more often. (Cf. `kink-ceilings.md` — repetition erodes intensity.)

## §2 — The 8 mechanical prose rules (Lane 1/2/3 RTS-flat)

Every Lane 1/2/3 scene body satisfies all 8. (Lane 4 capstones relax Rules 3/5/7 — see §4 Tier-3.)

### Rule 1 — Second-person voice
"You," not "she." The player is "you." The NPC is "he" / "Frank" / "him." Every Lane 1/2/3 body is in the
player's POV via the second-person pronoun.
> ✓ You take the stool. He slides a coffee across without looking up.
> ✗ She takes the stool. He slides a coffee across without looking up at her.

### Rule 2 — Stage-direction cap: 2 sentences per beat
A "beat" is one click that reveals new content (one `paragraph`, one `dialog`, or one cascade step).
Stage direction within a beat caps at 2 sentences. After two, drop a `dialog` beat or break to the next
click.
> ✓ You bend to load the dishwasher. He's at the counter, mug raised. *(2 sentences. Break.)*
> ✗ four sentences of business + window light + the kettle clicking, no dialog break

### Rule 3 — Zero environmental sensory detail
No smell, no window light, no kettle clicks, no dust motes, no coffee aroma. The HUD carries world state
(time, location, NPC heat) continuously — prose that paints the kitchen is doing world-grounding the HUD
already does, and pays for it on every re-read.
> ✓ You pour him coffee. He sets the paper down.
> ✗ The kitchen smells of coffee and damp wood. Sun catches the dust over the sink. You pour him coffee.

### Rule 4 — Dialogue does the character work, not narration
Character lives in what people say + how they say it, not in stage direction explaining who they are. The
flat register governs *density*, not whether people speak — where a beat is an exchange, carry it in
`dialog` blocks (their words, interruptions, refusals), never a narrated summary. **This is the
dialogue-MODE rule; it's stated in full in `references/lanes.md` "Voice register" — read it there.**
> ✓ [Frank] "Coffee's ready." / [You] "Thanks." / [Frank] "You eat?"
> ✗ Frank, gruff but secretly soft, gestured at the coffee, his voice carrying the weight of a man who…

### Rule 5 — No inferential prose
Surface-level only. The player gets the same observation the character gets — no narrator-inferred
backstory: no "the cup he keeps for her," no "the chair he added when she moved in." Inferential prose is
a **Tier-3 move, earned at capstones only** (§4) — on re-read in Lane 1/2/3 it's performative; you can't
keep "noticing the cup" thirty times.
> ✓ He pours her a coffee.
> ✗ He pours her a coffee — the same mug she'd reached for on day three, the chipped one he never tossed.

### Rule 6 — Direct/crude diction at sexual register
Crude is the default at the sexual register. "His cock." "Your cunt." "Your tits." "Your ass." Not "his
manhood," not "between your legs," not "your chest." Each NPC has a **per-tier vocabulary ceiling** (one
NPC goes full breeding-talk at the peak; another stays peer/school register) — declared in that NPC's design
brief, designed at `references/content-framework.md` §F ("Their voice and their ceiling"). Default to the
maximum-explicit reading when ambiguous; the ceiling caps how far, the rule says don't soft-pedal *below*
it.
> ✓ [Frank] "Open your mouth." / You go down on your knees. His cock against your face.
> ✗ His voice was low as he asked her closer. She felt herself responding, alive with something unnamed.

### Rule 7 — One beat = one click
Each click in a cascade reveals ONE narrative beat — one paragraph, or one dialog exchange, or one
image+caption. Don't pack multi-paragraph internal momentum into a single beat; the click pacing IS the
narrative pacing, and each click is a possible stat-tick moment.
> ✓ Beat 0 "He's at the counter. He looks up." → [click] Beat 1 "He sets the mug down. Quiet." → [click]
>   Beat 2 "He crosses the kitchen toward you."
> ✗ One click: "He's at the counter, looks up, sets the mug down, says 'Quiet,' crosses, takes your wrist."

### Rule 8 — Image-first composition
The visual asset carries the scene; prose is the ~30-word caption pinning what's happening, not a full
description that paints the image in words. This holds **even when the image is a Phase-1 placeholder** —
do NOT compensate for a missing visual with more prose (that's literary drift in disguise; the
placeholder IS the missing-image signal). Half of RTS scenes are 25 words or less — image-first is what
makes that work.
> ✓ [image: scenes/kitchen_morning.jpg] / [Frank] "Coffee?" / [You] "Yeah." — the image carries the room
>   + his pose + your POV; the prose pins the exchange.
> ✗ a 60-word paragraph repainting the warm light, the flannel, the two mugs, the softening face

The `[image: …]` above is **shorthand for this rule only** — the engine has no such syntax. Author the real
TOML media block (`{ type = "image", props = { file, description, search_queries } }`) with its
`search_queries` acquisition layer per `references/media.md`.

## §3 — Choice-label discipline

A hub choice **label is a terse action verb** — the action, not a sentence. The flavor lives in the
scene the click opens, NOT in the label (one-beat-one-click applied to the menu).
- Strip self-justifying subtext: `Square the crate 😏`, not `Square the crate. Ten minutes. Tell yourself
  it's just business.`
- **Crude word in the label at the ceiling.** At explicit tiers the verb is crude at the NPC's vocab
  ceiling: `Suck his cock`, not `Go down on him`.
- Labels are tighter than the prose. Emoji on hub/menu buttons (🔥 sex · 💬 talk · 🥃 pour · 💰 deal ·
  ↩️ leave); **bare** on in-loop cascade beats. (Full label/emoji rules: `references/lanes.md` Lane 1.)

## §4 — The three writing tiers

RTS doesn't write every scene at one density. Three tiers, assigned per a class of moment. **Tier ≠
length** — it's density. A 1,900-char capstone is Tier-3; a 10-word activity-pass is Tier-1.

| Tier | Used for | Function | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — utility one-liner** | sleep / nap / generic activity-pass; Lane 1 hub menu items | pure mechanical confirmation — text exists only to make the stat-tick feel like something | ~10 words |
| **Tier 2 — vignette** | Lane 2 ambients, Lane 3 substitution targets, anonymous-partner encounters | bridges mechanic to content — specific detail, RTS-flat structure, re-readable | ~30–50 words/beat, 2–4 beats |
| **Tier 3 — scripted character** | Lane 4 capstones; named-NPC intros, quest beats, arc transitions | real character writing — the layer that earns the game its narrative weight | bounded by the moment (~1,500–8,000 chars across a cascade) |

**RTS-flat is the Tier-1/Tier-2 default.** When it applies: every Lane 1 hub render, every Lane 2
ambient, every Lane 3 walk-in and its solo branch. Budget discipline is part of why a 130-scene game
ships at all — **don't spend Tier-3 prose on a Tier-1 moment.**

**Tier-3 is EARNED, capstone-only.** When it applies: Lane 4 once-only peaks (and the named-NPC
introduction that opens an arc). What Tier-3 earns that Lane 1/2/3 forbids:
- **Interior monologue + observation tied to memory** — the cumulative weight of past arc beats lands.
- **Inferential character work** ("the up-and-down a woman who'd hired forty waitresses did") — the Rule-5
  ban lifts here, *once*.
- **Layered detail per beat + composed rhythm** — sentences of varying length, deliberate cadence;
  Rule-7's one-beat-per-click relaxes to let momentum run across paragraphs.

What Tier-3 is **NOT**: not generic literary prose (it's specific to *this* scene's people + place, not
"the dim hallway in the quiet farmhouse"); not melodramatic (the prose stays controlled — the weight is
in the line, not underlined around it); not unlimited length (density is high, length is bounded by what
the moment needs). Writing a capstone at bland Lane-2 density wastes its once-only nature — that's the
inverse drift. Writing a Lane 2 ambient at Tier-3 grates on re-read — that's the common drift.

**The sanity check** before authoring any new body: *"Could this exact line appear in an RTS scene of the
same tier?"* If "RTS would never ship this — it reads like a literary novel," rewrite RTS-flat.

## §5 — The `thought_bubble` primitive

A real content block type — the private-thought glimpse. Renders as an italic bubble distinct from
speech: a 💭 glyph + "is thinking:" label, dashed border, muted color (`v2.py:13878` — `elif block_type == "thought_bubble"` render; player/NPC/unknown
speaker variants resolve through the same path, `v2.py:13885-13922` — `speaker = props.get(...)` branches). It is **orthogonal to the three
tiers** — it adds character interiority through a styled UI element, NOT through prose density, so the
surrounding prose stays RTS-flat.

```toml
{ type = "thought_bubble", props = { speaker = "npc", npcId = "npc_frank" }, content = "She doesn't know I watch her like this. Good." }
```

**Use it for** an NPC's charged interior on a Lane 2/3 ambient (adds his perspective without Tier-3
spillage — the bubble carries the interiority, the prose stays flat) and for the extra interiority
dimension on a Lane 4 capstone.

**Don't use it for** a Lane 1 utility menu item (one-line scene — a bubble is over-weight) or for the
player's own interior (the player's POV *is* the scene; thought-bubbles are for NPC interior — a
`speaker = "player"` variant exists in the engine but is reserved, not the default).
