# The content-question framework — the five subjects (the shared backbone)

The skill's content layer asks **what questions a game must answer to be whole**, organized by **subject**
(the unit a novelist holds in their head) — never by mechanism (lanes/tiers/gates). This one artifact runs
in **three touches**: Step 4 (`step-4-deep-design.md`) answers them as **story**, one subject at a time; Step 5
(`step-5-blueprint.md`) **blueprints** concrete, gated, placed scenes that align to them; Step 6
(`step-6-feedback.md`) **reviews** by running them against the finished game — an empty answer is a gap in
plain language, not a gate dump. All three read THIS file, so design, blueprint, and review can't drift.

**The questions are mechanism-free; their ANSWERS land on real engine knobs** (the bridge table at the
bottom) — a flag, a player/NPC meter, a gate, a canvas type — or they're prose that won't build. Keep the
bridge thin: it points at existing doctrine (`lanes.md` · `trait-design.md` · `sex-loop.md` · `systems.md`),
it doesn't re-teach it.

**Anti-double-count rule:** each cluster names its single OWNER and what merely DEFERS to it (the parentheticals
below). Honor them — they're what stop one scene being interrogated twice and dissolve the old Step-4/Step-5
overlap.

## §1 — The Fantasy & Shape (the whole game; answered ONCE, at Step 2)
*The establishing shot AND the arrival — both ends of the same desire ladder, plus the floor beneath it
(failure) and which systems this game uses. `step-2-toplevel.md` owns these.*

**A — The one hungry sentence**
- In one sentence a stranger would lean in to hear, what is the fantasy this game sells? Who are you, where are you, and what are you here to do?
- What is the single sharpest forbidden charge — the thing that makes a player's pulse jump reading the premise? Name the taboo and name the heat.
- Does the premise itself contain a beginning and an end — a 'come in low, climb to the top' shape — or is it just a situation with no built-in arc? Say the low and say the high.
- **Pressure-test the premise's internal logic — before you build a tower on it.** The engine builds an incoherent premise green; only a person reading it catches the hole, so catch it here at the premise, not after the arcs are stacked on top. Two the author reliably misses: (1) if the game hangs missions / factions / a villain / an economy off a named **institution** — a company, agency, cult, casino, estate — what does that institution actually **DO**: its function, its product, its purpose? Name it, or you've built a tower and a villain onto a blank ("what is this company even *about*?"). (2) If the premise is **infiltration / cover** — she passes as someone to reach a mark — does the cover **hold** against each mark: would this specific person **see through** her, recognize what she really is? An insider who'd clock her breaks the honeypot; recast the target as a deniable outsider, or change the cover. *(Vesper: the man who ran the facility that BUILT weapons like her would recognize one on sight — recast to an outside supplier who never knew what his gear was for.)*

**B — The desire ladder** (the player-facing spine, never a corruption bar)
- Walk the game as a chain of named wants, broke-and-open to the very top. At each step say what she WANTS right now, in her words, and what getting it makes her want next. No numbers — just the hungers in order.
- For every rung on that ladder, is there a real PAYOFF moment when she clears it — a scene she gets, not just a number ticking over? Point to any rung that has no payoff.
- Is the next want always VISIBLE before she can reach it — can she see the rung above her, greyed and named, so she always knows what she's climbing toward?
- If the bottom rung is a REACTIVE want — she's driven by something that already happened to her (revenge, escape, reclaiming what was taken) — is the CAUSE stated in the opening, the wound named, so the want reads as earned and not handed to her from nowhere? If instead the drive IS the appetite itself — pure hunger, a corruption sandbox where wanting more is the whole engine — it needs no backstory; don't bolt a tragedy onto it. Say which kind this ladder is, and if it's reactive, point to where the cause is spoken. *(Reactive models: `the_inheritance` anchors the revenge want in the estrangement; `last_call` anchors survival in the inherited debt — "Boyd's man comes Mondays. Two hundred."; `the_long_summer` restates the cause each time the want reframes. Pure-appetite, no wound: `under_one_roof`, `late_shifts`.)*

**C — The act shape, what each climb FEELS like, and whether failure exists**
- Sort the whole game into its big movements — the fall, the reach, the deepening. What is she DOING in each, and how does the texture of play change as she crosses from one to the next?
- When she crosses into a deeper act, what does that crossing FEEL like as a story moment — a line crossed, a door that opens, a new kind of thing she's now willing to do? Or does the meter just quietly tick up with nothing to mark it?
- Does the heat keep climbing forever, or does it climb hard and then widen into a place she lives in? Where does the steepest escalation stop and the livable plateau begin?
- **The fail-state declaration:** does failure exist at all — when she refuses a beat, neglects a person for days, lets a deadline lapse, runs out of money, does anything get worse, close off, or push back; or is forward the only direction? *(This is the single OWNER of "does failure exist." The ripples are traced in §4F; here just declare it — and if the answer is "no failure," say so on purpose, don't leave it true by default.)*

**D — The declared desire span** (chosen on purpose, no ambush)
- Who, exactly, is the player going to want across this whole cast — which genders, which kinds of dynamic, which registers of heat? Lay out the full spread the game promises, deliberately, so no scene blindsides a player with a tone they never signed up for.
- Does the cast you're about to design actually DELIVER every register you just promised, with at least one person carrying each — and is anyone carrying a register nobody asked for?
- Are there any hard lines this game will and won't cross — content the player should know is coming, or know is excluded, before they're inside a scene? Name what's IN and what's been cut.

**E — The opening, the player she names, and the systems in play** (the cold start)
- First screen of a brand-new game — nothing earned, no relationships, no money. What does she see, where is she standing, and what's actually happening around her?
- In that first session, what are the two or three things she can actually DO right away — before earning anything? And does at least one start nudging her downward without asking her to grind?
- What is the very FIRST want the game puts in front of her by name — the thing it tells her to go chase before she could possibly know the bigger game?
- How does the world TEACH her how to play without a tutorial — how does she learn what's possible, where to go, and that there's a climb ahead, just by looking at the screen?
- If a brand-new player only played for ten minutes, would they come away knowing what this game IS — a taste of the core charge, not just chores and setup?
- Is the player herself, or any NPC, customizable — her name, her body, her look, an NPC's name or relationship label? And wherever a customized value appears — prose, the quest card, a location name, a sidebar label, a phone contact — does the game actually speak it back, or print the raw default?
- Does the whole setup actually LAND in the opening prose itself — who she is, who the people around her are, the ties between them, the hook pulling her in — or would a player reading only the STORY arrive confused because the premise is parked in metadata, the Start/Customize screens, or a cascade button label? Read the cold open as a stranger who skipped the menus: are who-she-is and who-these-people-are NAMED on the page, plainly? *(Clarity, not flourish — state the cast and the ties flat; the opening stays terse, it just can't make the player infer the premise from UI. Models: `last_call` cold-reads "Sully's niece" in the opening; `the_long_summer`'s prologue names the cast; `the_inheritance`'s will-reading names each relationship in prose.)* *(That is the STORY half. The MACHINE
half — teaching navigation, the economy, the day-cycle, and what the HUD bands mean — is owned by
`references/onboarding.md`: a stranger who skipped the menus must also learn what's clickable and what the
sidebar says, surfaced in the opening, not inferred.)*
- **The systems ON/OFF declaration:** which optional engine systems does this game switch ON — phone, rent/debt clock, clothing wardrobe, customization — and which is it deliberately leaving OFF? Name each one in or out, so no system is half-wired by accident and none silently forgotten. *(Cite `systems.md`.)*

**F — The frontier** (the ladder's last rung)
- What is the single hottest, fully-earned moment at the very top of the game — the payoff the whole climb was for? Is arriving there an achievement, or does the screen just quietly run out of content?
- After that peak, can she keep PLAYING — is there a life to live at the top, with loops and a world and income still running, or does the game effectively stop?
- Does the top leave one visible thread pointing OUTWARD — a teased next adventure she can see but not yet take?
- At the very top, does the game TELL her honestly that she's reached the current peak and what to do now — or leave her guessing at an empty screen?
- Is the endgame the HOTTEST content in the game, or does it collapse into running a business — counting money, watching widgets, managing an empire on a dashboard? *(OWNER of the management-collapse test; §2C and §4E defer here.)*

## §2 — YOU: the player's own thread (Step 4 · Pass 1 — FIRST, before any NPC)
*The protagonist as her own subject — the blind-spot track. Everything that raises HER, populated at every
tier so the climb never dead-ends, plus the economy she lives, the items she buys, any non-depravity ladder
she climbs, and the anti-starvation audit. Authored BEFORE the NPCs so supply is sized before demand.*

**2A — The bootstrap: getting off zero with no one watching**
- On the very first night, alone in her own room with no one to perform for, what does she do to herself — and why (boredom, the rot starting inside, relief after a long day)?
- Is there anything she can do at corruption zero that isn't a solo act — some behavior or story moment that nudges her open before she's chosen to escalate at all?

**2B — The exhibition backbone: being SEEN** (the mid-tier workhorse)
- Once she's started to fall, where does she first dress bold and let herself be looked at — and what does she WANT out of being seen (tips, the thrill, proving her nerve, turning a specific head)?
- As her exhibition deepens, what's the bolder version of being-seen at each main stage — does the same place let her go from a daring outfit to actually putting herself on display?
- Does being-seen actually CHANGE how the world treats her — does her exposure set a state the world can read — or is it a number that ticks up with nothing reading it? *(How the world reacts is traced in §4/§5; here just confirm the state exists to be read.)*

**2C — Earning IS corrupting: the income ladder, the sinks, the items, the pressure**
- Walk her money story broke to rich: the lowest, most respectable way she earns at the start, the dirtiest best-paying thing by the end — and is the better money ALWAYS further down the lewd path?
- When one way of earning is blocked or she's sick of it, what ELSE can she do for money — and is each a real scene, not the same shift-button reskinned?
- What does she actually WANT to spend money on badly enough that she stays hungry even once she's earning well — the buys that keep the pressure alive?
- What KEY ITEMS or consumables does the money also buy that aren't clothing — a toy she teaches with, a drink/drug to loosen a target, a gift that moves an arc, a photo held as leverage, a key that opens a place? Name each, what it costs, what having it unlocks. *(What it unlocks is traced in §4.)*
- What's squeezing her for money, and does that squeeze get bigger and scarier across the game rather than fading once she's flush?
- When she takes over and runs the operation, does the income endgame stay HOT, not a dashboard? *(Owned by §1F — answer it there; here just confirm.)*

**2D — The ceiling and the other ladders she climbs**
- What is the most extreme, most public thing she does that's about HER and no single relationship — the top of her own depravity, unthinkable on night one?
- Early on, while she's still powerless and broke, can the world take a liberty she can't refuse — and does that stop as she rises to the top? *(The place that allows it and the recede are owned by §5/§4; here just say it exists on her own track.)*
- Besides corruption and exhibitionism, what OTHER tracks does she climb that aren't about depravity — a job rank, a skill, a fame/follower count, standing with a faction, a quest chain gated on flags or items? Walk each start to top and say what content ONLY it unlocks.
- Does her own track reach a livable top she can just LIVE in once the climb is done — her favorite loops still running — rather than going blank? *(The plateau is owned by §1F; here just confirm her solo loops survive it.)*

**2E — The supply-vs-demand check** (the anti-starvation audit — the OWNER of the count)
- Count her feeders band by band — is there at least one real thing to do at every step of the climb, or is some band, especially the deepest, nearly empty so the climb dead-ends?
- Now look at the deepest scenes the relationships demand — how far gone must she be for the hardest NPC content — and can an ordinary playthrough get her there through her own track, without grinding? *(Single OWNER of the supply-vs-demand count; §4E confirms, never re-counts.)*
- Read each feeder once more: does every paying or escalating thing she does answer to something she WANTS right now — or is there a row whose only reason to exist is to make a number go up?
- **Roster each solo activity as a host (the fused unit).** For every repeated solo activity — self-care (shower/bath/sleep/eat), exhibition, earning — name three things: the restore/want it serves, the player-lewd solo branch it carries (the feeder), and which NPC(s) walk in on it (the Lane 3 hijack). A self-care chore at a shared location that hooks into **nothing** (bare restore, no feeder, no walk-in) is the dead-bath gap — flag it here. The chore, the feeder, and the walk-in are ONE canvas (`references/lanes.md` Lane 3), so design them together, not as separate passes.

## §3 — THEM: the people (Step 4 · Pass 2 — one NPC at a time)
*Each NPC as a whole character, answered N times — plus, ONCE for the cast, the web between them. Empty cells
are honest: a peer gets no walk-ins, an ally no loop, an antagonist no vulnerability scenes. The per-NPC
answer is the story brief (`step-4-deep-design.md` Pass 2), structured into the scene list at Blueprint
(`step-5-blueprint.md` Pass 2).*

**A — Who they are, what THEY want, and how deep they go**
- Say this person in one charged line — who are they to you, and what is hot or forbidden about them? If you can't say it as a hungry sentence, they're not ready.
- What does THIS person want — independent of you? Name what they're chasing in their own life, so they can pursue, resist, scheme, or set terms instead of waiting for your meters to rise.
- How do they have teeth — where do they resist, push back, set a price, or almost out-play you? Or are they the willing one who comes to you?
- How deep is this person — a full, central figure, or a lighter flavor (a flirt, an ally, a one-note obstacle)? Be honest: not everyone earns a full arc.

**B — How you meet them** (the on-ramp: ungated, non-lewd, cold-start enterable — and a *designed first
encounter*, not a bare hub: `references/npc-intro.md`)
- In the very first session — fresh start, no stats, no flags — how do you first cross paths with this person, and is that first meeting something you can simply walk into?
- Is that first meeting clean of sex and clean of any other person's story — can you build the early bond through ordinary talk and presence, before any corruption and without needing another arc advanced first?

**C — Their arc start to end**
- Where does this arc END — paint the final, hottest state of this person at the peak. What do they become, and what does it look like?
- Walk the road from cold start to that end-state as a sequence of named turning points — the first warmth, the first crossing of a line, the first time, the surrender. The rungs, in order?
- What is the ONE thing that, as you build it, opens each new rung — and is it built by what you do WITH THEM specifically, not just by being globally slutty? And separately, where must YOUR own corruption be high enough to even attempt the most explicit beats?
- Once a turning point lands, how does THIS SAME person behave differently afterward — greeting, voice, new things possible, old moments retired? *(Whether anyone ELSE reacts is §4's job.)*

**D — Their everyday content as experience**
- What is the ladder of deliberate moves you CLICK to escalate this person — the menu of things you choose to do TO or WITH them, rung by rung? Read each: is this person the target of the verb?
- What do you CATCH this person doing when you walk into a room — the ambient glimpses of them living their own life, charged but bounded? Before and after they've fallen?
- While you're busy doing something solo — a shift, a shower, a quiet hour — does this person ever walk IN on you, intrude, surprise you mid-activity? Or is that not their role?
- Once they've warmed to you, do THEY ever come chasing — seek you out, invent reasons to be near you, escalate on their own without you clicking anything? How is that different from a glimpse you merely catch?
- Is there a scene where you're doing something solo and an act of yours moves THIS person's meter too — you catch them mid-act and it feeds your exposure and warms them, or a chore puts their hands on you? Name it, and say it counts toward BOTH your own track and theirs.

**E — Their milestones**
- What are the once-only BIG nights — first kiss, first time, surrender, breaking — the scripted peaks you reach once and never replay identically? Name each and what it sets in motion.
- For each big night, what exactly must be true before it can fire — how far this person has come AND, on the most explicit ones, how far down YOUR own road you are? Be precise about which lock is which, and never gate a once-only night on a heat meter that resets when they climax.

**F — Their voice and their ceiling**
- How does this person SOUND — diction, tells, what they call you, the private thought you glimpse? How does that voice shift as they fall?
- Is that voice delivered as the character's **own dialogue** — the player reads their words, their interruptions, what they refuse — or only narrated *about* them ("she tells you X")? Spoken agent vs narrated subject. Wherever there's an actual exchange, played is the default — hardest at the once-only peaks (`references/rts-flat-prose.md`, the played-dialogue rule); only a beat with literally no one present to speak (solo, unseen observation) stays narrated — if this person is *there*, they get a voice.
- How explicit do the scenes get at the peak — what acts are on the table, what is the crudest word the prose will use? Will the scene deliver it, or soft-pedal at the top? *(Whether a forced beat is allowed, and where, is settled by the place ceiling in §5B.)*

**G — The repeatable layer** (core arcs only)
- For a central person you keep coming back to: after the first big night, what repeatable thing can you DO again and again — the menu that keeps the hub from going hollow once the milestones are spent? *(Peripheral people get none — say so.)*
- Inside that loop, what is actually ON the menu — which acts and poses, how do they deepen as her corruption or his climbs, what are the finish options, and what makes a session with THIS person feel different from anyone else?
- What keeps that loop from going stale — how does it vary so it isn't the same click forever?

**H — The web between them, and what this arc SETS** (answered ONCE for the cast)
- Across the whole cast, who relates to whom independent of you — lovers, rivals, family, friends, allies to EACH OTHER? Name each pairing, and which is a scene the player witnesses, steps into, or can break.
- When this person's big moments land, what FACT about the world do they set that the rest of the game might care about — that you now own them, that they signed something over, that a door is now open, that their own want came true? Name the facts they leave behind; §4 traces who reads them.

## §4 — What Changes When: the reactivity web (Step 4 · Pass 4 — the wiring, LAST)
*The cross-cutting priority subject. For every state-change — hers, theirs, the world's — what becomes
DIFFERENT across the whole game: what opens, what shifts, what goes DARK, what it COSTS. The single place that
traces what every set-point declared elsewhere is READ BY — upward AND downward. Invents no new content; it
WIRES what Passes 1–3 created.*

**A — When YOUR own state crosses a line** (corruption tier, exposure, dominance)
- She just crossed from one band of depravity to the next. The instant that line is crossed, what is suddenly POSSIBLE that wasn't an hour ago? Name one new thing she can now DO, and one person now reachable differently.
- Take a hub you visit over and over. As her corruption climbs, does the OPENING paragraph ever read differently — or is it the CHOICES underneath that change while the greeting stays one constant paragraph? *(Catches tiering the constant opener on a stat instead of the choices.)*
- Is there a moment where her own descent changes how a person who is NOT the object of that scene treats her — she's gotten visibly more brazen and someone clocks it, unprompted?
- As she takes person after person and clearly becomes the one WINNING, does anyone react to her growing dominance over the whole house — an ally getting nervous, a bystander deferring — even though she never touched THEM? *(Distinct: the prior question reacts to her depravity; this one to her standing OVER others.)*

**B — When a person changes** (a stage flips, their own want comes true, a hidden meter climbs)
- Pick one NPC milestone. The morning after, walking back into their everyday hub, what is DIFFERENT — how they greet you, what they'll now do, what they say? List the everyday content that was dead before this night and alive after.
- When you finish breaking or winning one person, does anyone ELSE react — not to you, to what you did to THEM? Does the sister get easier because she heard, does one conquest light up a late rung in another story?
- When an NPC gets what THEY were chasing — lands the boy, closes the scheme, wins the promotion — what changes across the game? What opens, who's now in the room, what do they now let you do?
- Is there a hidden meter on anyone — a scheming, suspicion, or pressure track the player never sees — and as it silently climbs, what FIRES at each threshold? What colder behavior, new pressure beat, or threat lands without her tripping it?
- When you advance or own someone, does it ever take something AWAY from a THIRD person — cost the antagonist an ally, strip a pillar of power, retire a thread, shrink another's menace? For the big milestones, name not just what lights up but who now has LESS. *(Cross-NPC subtraction; the conquered person's OWN content changing is §3C's job.)*
- For every late beat that depends on another person's story being far enough along — when the player stands at that locked rung too early, does the game TELL them, in the fiction, exactly which other person to advance first? Or does it just sit greyed with no reason?

**C — When the outfit changes** (the clothing-driven reactive world)
- She puts on something far more revealing and walks her usual route — lobby, bar, a guest room, the deep end. Room by room, what does the world DO to her that it wouldn't in modest clothes? Where is the line between a room that only stares and a room where hands land? Is there an early window where the wrong place takes her with NO refusal — and does that danger RECEDE as she gains power, so the woman who runs the house is no longer prey to the same outfit in the same room?
- Does WHO is in the room change how far the outfit gets her — a predator escalating, a respectful person flustered, the schemer filing it away to use later? Same near-nothing dress: three different reads depending on who's standing there?

**D — When the wallet, the day, a place, or a body-state crosses a boundary**
- When you cross a threshold that hands you MONEY or someone's purse-strings, does an existing character's whole register toward you flip because you now hold their means — a price drops, someone starts paying, a debtor's tone changes? Name a money/leverage crossing and trace what it re-prices.
- Separately, when your RENOWN or standing crosses a band — you're suddenly someone with a name — whose register shifts for THAT reason alone, independent of money? *(Split from the above so a game rich in money-leverage can't score a false 'covered' while renown re-prices nothing.)*
- When a new day begins, or the hour turns afternoon-to-night, what content opens or closes that no stat governs — a night-only scene, a weekday-only person, a deadline one day closer? And when a new PLACE finally opens, what does its arrival ripple OUT to — new earning, a new place for someone to be, a new danger — or is it just one more room?
- Does this game carry a persistent BODY-STATE that branches content across the cast — pregnancy, lactation, addiction, a permanent mark, a used/broken status? If so, when active, what new scenes open, what existing scenes change, and how does she enter and leave it?

**E — The machine as one living thing** (does it all wire together)
- Walk the whole engine out loud as a circuit: working earns money and reputation, which buys what, which lets you reach whom, whose conquest funds the next, all the way to the top. Does that circle actually CLOSE — does the income that funds the final conquest come from a scene the player really plays?
- Is there any meter that climbs all game and never opens a single door — a number that feels like progress but gates nothing? Any flag set but never the reason anything happens? *(Reads every track incl. the §2D non-corruption ladders and the group-B hidden accumulators; the player-feeder-vs-NPC-floor balance is owned by §2E — confirm here, don't re-count.)*
- Lay the dependency map flat: who needs whom advanced first. Is it ever circular — A needs B while B needs A — so the player hits an impassable wall? And is any FIRST meeting hidden behind another person's progress?
- Take the single hottest beat. After it fires and the house is owned, what's still PLAYABLE the next morning, and does the game honestly tell the player they've reached the current peak? *(Frontier shape + management-collapse owned by §1F; here only confirm the frontier flag reads into a livable steady-state + a greyed next-hook, not a dead end.)*

**F — When she loses ground** (the negative axis — the consolidated stress-test)
- What happens when the ticking clock actually RUNS OUT or a deadline is missed — the rent unpaid, the foreclosure landing, the antagonist's legal move succeeding? Does the world react to her LOSING ground, or does the pressure only ever escalate in the background and never actually bite?
- When she neglects a person for days, or refuses an escalation again and again, does anything regress, cool, or close — or does every state simply freeze, untouched, until she comes back?
- *(Defer pointers: the high-level "does failure exist at all" declaration is owned by §1C; the place-specific downside outcomes of risky exposure — caught, robbed, fined, an item stolen — are owned by §5C. This group traces whether the world as a whole reacts to her losing ground. If §1C declared "no failure by design," answer that here in one line and move on.)*

## §5 — The World (Step 4 · Pass 3 — the stage)
*The map and the clock as content substrate, plus the surfaces that aren't places — the phone channel and the
HUD. What each place is FOR, how far it lets the world go, who reacts in it, when anyone is there, the cadence
and pressure clock, and how the player can tell where to go.*

**5A — The map: what each place is FOR** *(the room-content gate — `location-design.md` §6)*
- Walk me through every place the player can stand in. For each, finish with no hedging: 'This place exists so the player can ___.' **This is a GATE, not a prompt: a navigable (non-container, non-offscreen) place that can't name one clear thing it's for does not ship — cut it, or give it a solo activity so it earns the click.** A reachable empty room is the dead-room defect (The Inheritance's dead kitchen/bath/terrace); the build never catches it, so this question is the catch. (A deliberately-locked deferred shell is exempt while its unlock beat is on the roadmap.)
- Sort your places into kinds: the earning-and-being-seen stage, the private room where one person opens up, the lawless deep end where anything goes, the shop where money goes to die, the shared hub everyone passes through. Does every kind exist, or is one missing?
- For each place, which track lives there — her own solo/earning turf, one NPC's home ground, or both? Name a place where the work she does ALONE and the person she works ON share a room — and confirm those stay separate things to do, not blurred into one menu.

**5B — The ceiling: how far each place lets the world go** (OWNER of where forced content is allowed)
- Stand in each place in the boldest outfit. What is the worst — or hottest — thing that can happen to her here, and what simply can't, no matter how she's dressed? In one place a hand on her back is the ceiling; in another she gets used on the spot. Name that ceiling for every place.
- Which places allow the no-choice, can't-refuse liberties — the prey-floor stuff — and which never do? And is that allowance tied to WHEN in the game we are, so it fades as she stops being prey? *(Single OWNER of where forced content is allowed and how it act-scopes out.)*

**5C — The reactive public and its risks** (OWNER of the place-specific downside outcomes)
- In the public places, who reacts to her besides the named cast — guests, regulars, staff, people on the street? When she walks in dressed bold, who notices and what do they DO about it?
- Among that public, who's the predator and who's the prude — the same low-cut top making one escalate and another go red? Name the dispositions so the reaction isn't one flat 'everyone gropes her.'
- Take ONE outfit and walk it through three different rooms. Tell me the three different scenes it triggers. If it's the same scene in all three, what's wrong?
- What goes WRONG when she pushes too far in the wrong place — caught, robbed, taken as punishment, fined, an item stolen, a reputation hit? Name the real downside outcomes of risky exposure and what they COST — or is exposure pure upside? *(Owner of place-specific downsides; the whole-world "reacts to her losing ground" tracing is §4F.)*

**5D — The clock: who is here, and when**
- Pick any place and any hour. Who's standing there? Move to a different hour — who's there now, who's gone? If a place has the same people at every hour, is time doing any work?
- For each person, where do they go when the player CAN'T follow — to sleep, a job offscreen, home for the night? Or do they blink out of existence when their hub closes?
- For the people she lives with, is "is he home right now" a fact her own routines roll against — does showering or cooking sometimes mean one of them walks in? Their home presence is the tripwire the routine fires on, not décor; for core housemates keep it a reachable, room-resolved presence, not an offscreen "away" label (`references/lanes.md` Lane 3 · `references/location-design.md`).
- How does a day actually PASS for the player — how does she get from afternoon to night, today to tomorrow? And does every scene that needs a time-of-day sit in a phase she can travel to, awake, with energy to spare?
- Which scenes are night-only, weekday-only, after-hours? Name the times that gate content — is that gating doing dramatic work, or just realism noise?

**5E — Cadence & pressure**
- How many days from the first nervous thing to the deepest thing — is the climb measured in days and small steps, or can a determined player rush it in one sitting? What stops her from maxing a person out in an afternoon?
- What's the ticking clock hanging over all of this — the deadline, the debt, the threat that makes the days urgent? When does it hit, and what escalates it as the game goes on? *(Whether it BITES on expiry is §4F.)*

**5F — The phone & the sidebar** (the surfaces that aren't places)
- Is there a phone or off-location channel, and what content lives THERE that lives nowhere else — chat threads that advance as people fall, a photo-sale/sexting economy, leverage held over a screen, daily texts that change tone after a milestone? Or is the phone deliberately absent? *(If it exists, its threads fire only on flags + elapsed days — never a place, time, or random roll. `systems.md`.)*
- What does the player SEE on the sidebar that helps her plan — does an NPC panel show where each person is, how warm they are, the named next step with its place and time-window? And which internal meters must stay HIDDEN because surfacing them — a scheming track, an awareness count, a stage label — would spoil the game?

**5G — Access & travel**
- On the very first day, which places can she walk into, and which are shut? For each shut place, what opens it — and can she SEE it's there and locked, or does it just not exist until it appears?
- How does the player get from home to town and back? One connected space or two worlds she travels between — and does leaving any room drop her somewhere geographically sensible?
- When a place opens because of something she did, does the game tell her WHY it opened and what to do there next? Or does a new door just quietly appear and hope she notices?

**5H — Shared private space (who can be seen / heard / walked in on)**
- Take each shared private space from the clock (§5D) — the one bathroom, a bedroom, a changing room — where someone is scheduled doing something private (showering, sleeping, changing) and the player can be next door. Is THIS place one where she can catch them showering (the peek), find it occupied (the room itself names who's in there), and get caught showering in there herself? *(The player is always a party — she watches, she's watched, or she's in it; two NPCs with the player absent is not a scene.)*
- This is a **per-place, user-confirmed call (`run-mode.md` Mode A)** — surface the candidate ("the bathroom, where Frank showers 7–7:30") and confirm it's in play BEFORE authoring; never a game-wide "add peeping" toggle, never silent. A game with no such spaces raises nothing. Honor the place's ceiling (§5B).
- *(The mechanism is the `npc_at_location` occupancy predicate; the authoring patterns are `references/lanes.md` Lane 3 shared-space. Two NPCs observed together is deferred engine work — leave it out for now.)*
